## 2. Scoring convention (ours, stated)

IG1230 Table 4 grades five task groups (Execution, Awareness, Analysis, Decision, Intent) as P (people) / P/S / S (system) per level. A contextualisation's score is **the highest level whose five row requirements are all met**, **+0.5** when the Awareness *and* Analysis rows already meet the next level's requirement. *(Annotated 2026-09-24, §6.3: this is an unweighted special case of IG1252's own **Scoring method 2** (§5.3.2.2.4, Lbase + Lextra), which E-2026-09-19-36 had not read; the scores in §3 are Method-2-approximate and not comparable to a Method-1 average. Re-score per act A15.)* This makes the scale's bias visible rather than hiding it: IG1230 raises Execution to S at L2 and Decision to S at L4, so **a layer that by design leaves decision and actuation with a human cannot score above L3 on a class-3 task and above ~L1.5 on a class-4 task, whatever its detection quality.** That is the guardrail ("autonomous oversight, not control") priced in the industry's own currency — and it is the point of scoring rather than asserting.

Two scores per contextualisation: **specified** (what ADR-002 and its sibling ADRs specify the layer to be) and **as-built today** (what exists on 2026-09-19: no bearer-facing agent is deployed; ADR-002 item 5's eval harness and ADR-007 item 1's replay corpus are open; the register-facing agents run in this engagement's daily loop as tool-assisted drafting reviewed by a human).

## 4. What the number says, and what it does not

1. **The asserted claim is corrected.** ADR-002 ladder paragraph (b) said "L3 on IG1230's scale, with L4 awareness and analysis". Scored, the *specified* layer is **2.6** — L2–L3 — and the *as-built* layer is **1.3**. The per-row statement in (b) was right (Awareness/Analysis at L3–L4, Decision at L3 for classes 1–2 and pinned at P for classes 3–4); the one-number summary "L3" was not, because IG1252 averages the flows the guardrail deliberately holds down. ADR-002 (b) is restated on this score (E-2026-09-19-46).
2. **The low Optimization score is the guardrail, not a defect.** 1.5 is what "the agent advises, never actuates" costs on a scale that rewards automated execution. Raising it means moving actuation into the machine, which R4/ADR-002 forbid for class-4 objects. This is the number to show anyone who reads "L3 oversight layer" as a promise of an L3 network.
3. **The Inventory flow is the only one with as-built evidence, and it is this register.** Its 2.25 is earned by the daily loop (drafts system-written, every source read by a human, the ARB deciding) — the honest reading is that the engagement is already running the class-1–2 pattern it specifies, at L2–L2.5, with the dissent/accuracy metrics of ADR-010 not yet measured on it.
4. **The gap between 2.58 and 1.31 is the build.** It closes through ADR-007 item 1 (replay corpus), ADR-002 item 5 (harness), ADR-007 item 2 (shadow-on-legacy) — in that order, because IG1252 scores production flows and nothing bearer-facing exists to score until shadow mode runs.
5. **TM Forum's own L3/L4 line corroborates the convention (E-2026-09-19-47).** IG1218F v2.0.0 Table 2 defines L3 self-configuration as "automatically delivered and manually reviewed" and L4 as "eliminating the need for manual review"; IG1339 v2.4.0 §4.3.1.3 keeps "manual review is required for certain ones" inside its L4 fault-management capability. So the +0.5 ceiling below L4 for every gated contextualisation is the industry's definition, not only ours.
6. **Not scored:** the Intent/Experience row beyond P (no intent interface exists until the TMF921/28.312 form of the ladder is built — ADR-002 item 9); cross-domain contextualisations (ADR-007 item 7); anything on the RU side.

### 6.2 What the mapping changes in the reading of this file's scores

1. **The Maintenance rows scored three contextualisations of two tasks.** Rows (1)–(3) exercise *fault identification* and *fault demarcation* — the Awareness and Analysis groups. They do not score the Intent group (policies generation, fulfilment evaluation), impact analysis, prediction, or the three recovery tasks. GB1523B, by IG1252's own construction, scores all ten. **A validation on GB1523B would therefore score the register on seven tasks this file never claimed a level for.** Where the honest answer is "no bearer-facing agent exists" the score is 1.0; where the answer is "the human does it by decision" the score is P — and IG1252 §5.3.2.2.3 says the criterion at every level "is to consider what agents, human, machine, or both, are performing the task", so a human-by-design task scores as a human task, not as a gap.
2. **Two tasks the guardrail does *not* pin at P were scored as if it did.** *Intent fulfilment evaluation* and *service verification* are observational: they confirm that a recovery — whoever executed it — cleared the interruption. Nothing in ADR-002 or ADR-004 forbids a system doing that. This file's row convention collapsed Execution into one P for classes 3–4; IG1252's split (E1 implement / E2 verify, §5.3.2.2.3) would let E2 score S without touching the guardrail. That is a legitimate, honest increase in the achievable Maintenance score, and it should be taken at the re-score.
3. **Fault impact analysis is the task that connects the score to the ontology work.** Its IG1252 description is "affected base stations and home broadband users"; the rail equivalent is *which sections of line, which RBC areas, which trains on which paths*. That is answerable only with the cell→section term (ibn-core pivot §4 item 3) and a `Path` object (path-booking note finding 2). The knowledge-plane gap of ADR-002 item 9 is, in ANLET terms, a Level ceiling on one specific task.
4. **The 23-June class of event is two scenarios, not one.** By §5.3.2.2.2 selected scenarios are independent and classified by fault type; a core-network "silent fault after planned change" is a GB1523B fault-type scenario *and* a GB1524B change-type scenario (change verification and fallback plan). The register's replay corpus (ADR-007 item 1) should be built as two scenarios so that a future validation can be run against either questionnaire.

### 6.3 The scoring-method finding — §2's convention is an unweighted special case of IG1252 Method 2

§2 of this file called its convention "ours, stated so it can be disputed". IG1252 §5.3.2.2 in fact defines two scoring methods, which E-2026-09-19-36 had not read:

- **Method 1 (§5.3.2.2.3):** each task scores the highest level it meets; a scenario is the weighted average of its tasks; the object is the weighted average of its scenarios. IG1252's own IP-fault-management example (Table 5-18, equal weights): scenarios 3.51 / 3.01 / 2.38 → object **2.96**.
- **Method 2 (§5.3.2.2.4):** a scenario's *basic* level Lbase is the highest integer level **all** its tasks meet; the *extra* level Lextra is the weighted fraction of tasks meeting Lbase + 1 (example weights A 10 %, B 20 + 15 %, C 10 + 10 %, D 15 %, E 10 + 10 %); Lscenario = Lbase + Lextra. The same example: 2.85 / 2.7 / 1.9 → object **2.6**.

**The two methods differ by 0.36 on IG1252's own example.** This file's rule — "the highest level whose five rows are all met, +0.5 when Awareness *and* Analysis already meet the next level" — is Method 2 with Lbase computed over the five *groups* instead of the tasks, and Lextra fixed at 0.5 for one specific pair of groups instead of weighted over every task that meets the next level. Consequences: (i) the specified **2.58** and as-built **1.31** are Method-2-shaped numbers and are *not comparable* to a Method-1 figure; (ii) the fixed +0.5 undercounts where Intent or Execution-verification also meet the next level (see 6.2 item 2) and overcounts where only one of Awareness/Analysis does; (iii) IG1252's note under Table 5-16 — "evaluation criteria should refer to specification developed by network domain specific SDO … users or other SDOs may have their own rules" — is the licence for a stated convention, but it also says the criteria for a *rail* bearer do not exist: no rail SDO has written level criteria for fault management on a safety-critical bearer, and GB1523B's are TM Forum's for a CSP core. That is the L5–L3 void of `pivot-notes-2026-09-19-ontology.md` seen from the scoring side.

**Consequence for this file:** the scores stand as *Method-2-approximate*, labelled so in §2 (annotated 2026-09-24). The re-score under trigger (e) is now specified as act **A15**: Method 2 proper, on Table 5-6's ten tasks with IG1252's example weights until GB1523B's own task list and weights are held, E1/E2 split, Intent tasks scored, the 23-June corpus as two scenarios. No number in §3 is changed today; changing a score without the questionnaire would be the convention problem repeated.

**3GPP's position, read the same day (E-2026-09-24-04, TR 28.909 V18.0.0 §4.1, §7.1).** SA5 separates the *qualitative* level — read per task from "the participation of the human and telecom system", against the standardised task list of TS 28.100 — from the *quantitative* score (ANLS), and concludes that the score "depends on the evaluator's specific evaluation purpose and concrete implementation … thus ANLS does not need to be normalized in 3GPP". Two consequences for this file: the stated-convention approach is what both SDOs expect, provided the convention is stated (now done); and the qualitative reading — which *is* standardised — has to be made against TS 28.100's fault-management task list as well as IG1252 Table 5-6, which the register cannot do until 28.100 is held (B22). TR 28.909 also supplies the effectiveness measure that goes beside the level: per-phase time cost of each fault recovery (recognition · demarcation · root cause · mechanism analysis · action evaluation and determination · execution), faults meeting the operator's recovery-time expectation, and total faults — A15 step 15.6.

### 7.3 Arithmetic (IG1252 §5.3.2.2.3 and §5.3.2.2.4 — both, since 2026-09-25)

**What GB1059 adds (E-2026-09-25-01).** ANLET's input is **one level per capability**: each question offers two to four options, each option carries a score, and in the worked example the options are the levels themselves (A = L4 … D = L1). The tool then applies "comprehensive calculation functions which take into account the weights if included"; the function itself is in the questionnaire tool (GB1523B; not held — *corrected 2026-09-26, E-2026-09-26-01: GB1059A is the RAN fault-management questionnaire, not a template*). One level per capability, weighted, is the input **Method 1** (§5.3.2.2.3) consumes directly — a weighted average. **Method 2** (§5.3.2.2.4) is computable from the same per-row levels. Until GB1059A says which function the tool runs, this section computes **both**, unweighted and weighted, and reports four figures per object and scenario.

For each of the two objects (layer specified · layer as-built · bearer), per scenario: mark each row 1 or 0 at each level L0–L5 from the criterion column and the holder column; **Lbase** = highest level at which every row is 1; **Lextra** = Σ weight × [row meets Lbase + 1]; **Lscenario = Lbase + Lextra**; object = Σ scenario weight × Lscenario. Rows held by a human *by design* (1, 9; 10 for the layer) are scored as human tasks at the level the human performs them, per §5.3.2.2.3. **The numbers are not computed in this sitting** — act A15 steps 15.2–15.5 — so that the first Method-2 figure is produced beside the §3 figures in one table, not scattered.

### 7.6 GB1059 read — what the auditor's method says about this section (added 2026-09-25, E-2026-09-25-01)

**1. Confirmation, and its limit.** GB1059 §3 builds the Core Network Fault Management questionnaire on TS 28.100 §7.3 (scenario), §7.3.1 (capabilities) and §7.3.2 (options and scores), plus TS 28.104 §8.4.3 for MDA-assisted fault management. §7.1's rows and criterion column are that construction. The limit is exact: ANLET's *questions* attach an implementation description and a **coverage threshold** to each level ("cover **% or more fault management scenarios"); GB1059 prints the placeholders, GB1523B fills them. §7.1 has neither the wording nor the thresholds, so it can place a capability at a level by 28.100's criterion but cannot reproduce the *pass test* GB1523B applies. That is the residual for B21.

**2. The option ladder is the implementation-class rule, seen from the auditor's chair.** GB1059's root-cause example grades by *how* the task is done: **A (L4)** AI-model-based diagnosis · **B (L3)** a fault-diagnosis knowledge model ("object state relationship model") · **C (L2)** expert rules "in the form of scripts or fault decision trees" · **D (L1)** manual, from the alarm-handling manual. ADR-002's implementation-class rule (decided 2026-08-20) confines any component on a class-3 or class-4 path to fully verifiable implementations and admits learned components in classes 1–2 only under conditions. Read together: **on the safety bearer's fault-management path, the register's designed answer to this question is C (L2)** — rule sets and decision trees are the DZSF "vollständig prüfbar" class (E-2026-08-19-02) — with **B (L3)** arguable where the state-relationship model is itself verifiable, and **A (L4) excluded by design, not by capability.** §4 item 2 said the low score is the guardrail priced; here is the price per question — one rung, at the point where ANLET's ladder switches from rules to learned models. This does not change any score in §3 (those rows scored *who* does the task, not *how*); it is the reading to give an auditor who asks why the register would decline option A.

**3. Version to score against.** GB1059 cites TS 28.100 **v17.1.0** and places root-cause analysis "for certain network faults" at L2; TS 28.100 **V19.0.0** §7.3.2 (held, E-2026-09-24-05) places Task H at L3 and applies "for certain network faults" to Tasks F, I and J. §7.1 follows V19.0.0 and says so in each row. A comparison with a GB1523B result must first establish which 28.100 release GB1523B v2.2.0 was written to.

**4. Two objects, again.** ANLET grades "the core network" — the bearer. The oversight layer is, in 28.100's words, the bearer's *authorized consumer*; ANLET has no column for it. §7.1's three holder columns keep both readings; only the bearer column is the one an ANLAV validation would ever produce.
