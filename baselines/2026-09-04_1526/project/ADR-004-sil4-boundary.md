# ADR-004: SIL-4 boundary — agent/kernel separation

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-06-24
**Deciders:** Architecture Review Board · NSA / safety authority liaison · Safety case lead (CENELEC) · Vpnet engagement lead
**Depends on:** ADR-002 (agentic decision & oversight layer); underpins ADR-003 (EU AI Act classification)
**Affects requirements:** R11 (keep the safety case certifiable — deterministic SIL-4 kernel outside the learning agents), with bearing on R9 (autonomy as a separate layer) and R10 (human oversight proportional to risk)

> **Scope & honesty note.** This ADR defines the **boundary design** and the **assurance argument** for keeping the learning agents outside the SIL-4 safety perimeter, grounded in the CENELEC EN 5012x framework and the EU AI Act Article 3(14) "safety component" test. It does **not** claim the *verifying evidence* yet exists: the independence / freedom-from-interference (FFI) analysis, the hazard log, and the independent (ISA) / Notified-Body assessments are **action items still to be produced** in detailed design. The specific EN 5012x / EN 50159 provisions cited below are used at framework level and must be **verified against the standard text and logged as A-tier evidence** before the safety case relies on them (action item 4). This moves R11 from *boundary-undefined* to *boundary-designed* — not to *evidenced*.
>
> **Standards-basis update (2026-08-01, E-2026-08-01-10/-11/-13).** The railway-software standard cited throughout as "EN 50128" is **superseded by EN 50716:2023** ("Railway Applications — Requirements for software development"), which unifies EN 50128 (control/protection software) and EN 50657 (rolling-stock software). Read every "EN 50128" reference below as **EN 50716:2023** for any current-regime work; EN 50128:2011 remains lawful only for pre-amendment transition regimes. The current safety standard set is **EN 50126-1/-2:2017 · EN 50716:2023 · EN 50129:2018+AC:2019 · EN 50159:2010+A1:2020**. Crucially, these standards are **not a legal mandate** but a **three-layer route**, verified against primary law: (1) the CCS TSI Table A 3 cites them by transition regime (base 2023/1695 → EN 50128:2011; as amended by Reg (EU) 2026/693 → EN 50716:2023); (2) Table A 3 is a **non-exclusive safe harbour** into the CSM-RA process — applying it is "an appropriate means," and alternative standards are permitted where "at least equivalence shall be proven"; (3) EN 50716:2023 is the **OJ-harmonised** standard giving presumption of conformity under Art. 17 of Dir (EU) 2016/797 (CID (EU) 2025/424, row 114a, since 2025-03-06), while **EN 50128's presumption has lapsed** (absent from the current harmonised list). The assurance argument should therefore rest on EN 50716 + the safe-harbour/equivalence structure, **never** on language of "mandatory compliance."

## Context

ADR-002 places learning agents (Risk Sentinel, Assurance, decision-support) **over** the FRMCS bearer as autonomous **oversight, not control**. R11 makes the certifiability of that arrangement load-bearing: the safety case is only defensible if the certified SIL-4 core is provably isolated from the agents, so agent behaviour cannot affect a safety function. Two guarantees ride on the *same* separation:

- **Safety (R11 / CENELEC).** Under EN 50126 (RAMS lifecycle), EN 50128 (software) and EN 50129 (safety-related electronic systems / safety case), a SIL-4 function carries a tolerable hazard rate on the order of 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁸ per hour. EN 50129's **composition** principle is decisive: a function's safety integrity cannot exceed that of any component it depends on. If a SIL-4 function depended on an agent output, the agent would inherit SIL-4 — infeasible for non-deterministic, learning components.
- **Regulatory (ADR-003 / EU AI Act).** The "not high-risk" finding (E-2026-06-24-07, tier A) rests on Article 3(14): the layer is not a *safety component* because its outputs are advisory and its failure does not endanger safety. That holds **only while** the separation below holds.

The question is live, not academic: E-2026-06-24-17 records **CTMS**, DB's AI-based Capacity & Traffic Management System that plans and part-automates train movements — a real in-domain AI traffic-decision system that *raises* exactly this boundary question. ADR-002's whole credibility depends on answering it precisely. Update (E-2026-07-02-14): DB has since answered this question for CTMS itself, in its own words — the CTMS "is not being designed as a safety-critical system"; safety responsibility lies with the interlocking-operating system (APS, on the Safe Computing Platform). The in-domain flagship AI thus adopts exactly this ADR's two-domain pattern: the learning system plans within the vital layer's constraints but holds no safety authority.

## Decision

**Proposed:** Adopt a **two-domain architecture with a one-way safety boundary**.

**Vital domain (SIL-4, certified).** The deterministic safety kernel holds the **sole** safety authority and the **sole** actuation capability over safety-critical functions (movement-authority enforcement, emergency braking, interlocking logic, ETCS vital functions). Engineered and assessed to SIL-4 under EN 50126/50128/50129. It is **unchanged by the presence of the agents**.

**Advisory domain (non-vital, agentic).** The ADR-002 agents consume telemetry and produce **advice and alerts to humans** (and, at most, to non-safety planning/operations systems). They have **no command path** into the vital domain.

Three separation properties are enforced **by construction**, not by procedure:

1. **Unidirectional information flow at the boundary.** The advisory domain may *read* vital-domain state (telemetry/observability) but cannot *write* into the vital safety path. The vital gateway accepts only human-authenticated, vital-protocol commands and structurally rejects agent-originated commands. Candidate mechanisms — a data-diode / unidirectional gateway on the read path; the vital command path kept physically/logically separate and protected per **EN 50159** (safety-related communication) — are a P0 detailed-design output; the **unidirectionality property itself is the decision**.
2. **Human-in-command interposition (couples to R10).** No agent output reaches a safety actuation except through a human decision (the R10 HITL gate). The agent advises; a competent human commands; the vital kernel enforces. **The agent cannot close the loop** — the human, not the agent, is the decision authority for any safety-relevant action.
3. **Freedom from interference / independence (EN 50129).** Where advisory and vital functions share any resource (compute, network, timing), spatial and temporal partitioning must be demonstrated **to the SIL of the vital function**; otherwise the agent inherits SIL-4. **Preferred realisation: physical segregation** (distinct hardware and network), so FFI is argued by segregation rather than by a fragile partitioning case.

**Governing principle (EN 50129 composition):** the safety case must show that **no vital function depends, for its integrity, on any agent output.** The agent is strictly *additive*. Its failure modes — wrong or hallucinated advice, unavailability, adversarial manipulation — must fall in the "no effect on the safety function" class, because the vital kernel and the human retain sole authority.

**Mandated precedent for the layering logic (E-2026-08-01-15).** The same "non-vital, separable, outside the safety re-approval scope" pattern is already standardised and CCS-TSI-mandated in ERTMS itself: UNISIG **SUBSET-146 §3.1.1.6** deliberately separates the **security layer** (TLS) from the **safety layer** so that the security layer can be updated *"without the need to re-approve any safety rating of the application."* This is the direct architectural cousin of the boundary here — a non-vital layer engineered to be updatable and composable-out of the safety case. It supports the Option-A framing ("keep the certifiable path small; the additive layer is separable by construction") with a primary, in-law example, and it gives the automation-bias/HITL work a clean model: the vital safety layer is the fixed, certified core; everything above it — security *and* the advisory agents — is separable, updatable, and outside the SIL-4 re-approval perimeter, provided the one-way boundary and composition properties hold.

## Assurance argument (evidence to be produced)

- **Hazard analysis** enumerating agent-failure modes, explicitly including **automation bias** — the residual path by which an *advisory* system can still contribute to harm if a human over-trusts its output and issues an unsafe command. This couples R11 to R10: the boundary holds only if the human is a genuine decision-maker, not a rubber stamp. R10's human-oversight design must actively counter automation bias (decision-class gating, surfacing uncertainty, no auto-accept).
- **Independence / FFI analysis** (EN 50129) and **safety-related communications** assessment (EN 50159) for the boundary.
- **Dependency demonstration**: no vital function's safety argument cites an agent output.
- **Independent assessment (ISA)** and, per the CCS TSI, **Notified-Body EC verification** for any constituent touching the certified perimeter.

## Link to ADR-003 (EU AI Act)

The Article 3(14) carve-out (not-high-risk) holds **iff** these properties hold: the agent is non-actuating and its failure does not endanger safety. The FFI + human-in-command + composition argument is *precisely* what keeps the agentic layer outside the safety-component perimeter. The same boundary evidence therefore serves both the safety case (R11) **and** the regulatory classification (ADR-003). Any breach — the agent gains an actuation path, or a vital function comes to rely on agent output — re-triggers Annex I §B high-risk.

## Options considered

### Option A — Segregated two-domain, unidirectional boundary, human-in-command (recommended)
| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Complexity | Moderate — distinct hardware/network, vital gateway |
| Cost | Higher hardware (physical segregation) |
| Safety/assurance | Strongest — FFI argued by segregation; clean composition story |
| Reversibility | High — agent is additive; can be removed without touching the vital case |

Pros: simplest, strongest independence and Art 3(14) argument; agent failure provably no-effect. Cons: more hardware; puts real weight on R10 (automation bias).

### Option B — Shared platform with software partitioning (mixed-criticality)
| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Complexity | High — temporal/spatial partitioning to SIL-4 on a platform hosting ML |
| Cost | Lower hardware, higher assessment burden |
| Safety/assurance | Fragile — partitioning case for non-deterministic components is hard |
| Reversibility | Lower |

Pros: less hardware. Cons: must argue partitioning to SIL-4 for a platform co-hosting learning components; heavy, brittle FFI case. Possible fallback only if segregation proves impractical.

### Option C — Agents issue (revocable) commands into the vital path with kernel veto
| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Complexity | High |
| Cost | — |
| Safety/assurance | Unacceptable |
| Reversibility | Low |

Cons: gives the agent an actuation path → it **becomes a safety component** (Art 3(14) high-risk per ADR-003) and must be built to SIL-4 — infeasible for learning components. **Rejected.** This is the anti-pattern the boundary exists to prevent.

## Trade-off analysis

The governing trade is **assurance strength/simplicity vs platform cost**. Option A's physical segregation costs more hardware but yields the strongest, simplest independence/FFI argument and the cleanest Art 3(14) story. Option B trades hardware for a much harder safety and assessment case. Option C is rejected on both safety and regulatory grounds simultaneously — it collapses the very separation that R11 and ADR-003 depend on. **Grounding (E-2026-07-02-01 — DB × Siemens Mobility "SIL4 Data Center" research report, 2021, on the RCA/OCORA Safe Computing Platform):** the industry reference for exactly this certified vital domain, and it independently supports the Option-A preference — it reports that cloud-like **dynamic resource management directly affects the safety case and cannot easily be met by non-safety-relevant cloud software**, and that mixed-criticality integration/homologation complexity rises dramatically. This is a **design-basis** anchor (RCA/OCORA + EN 5012x homologation), NOT the engagement's own FFI verifying evidence (action items 2/4/5 stand). The SIL4-Cloud follow-on (E-2026-07-02-03 — Thales/SYSGO/Fraunhofer/ESE/DB, 2022) refines the Option-B view: a certified **separation kernel** (SYSGO PikeOS, avionics-derived) makes **deterministic** mixed-criticality (SIL-4 + non-SIL) partitioning mature — but it does **not** cover **learning/non-deterministic** components, and its own residual challenges (dynamism, systematic failures in non-SIL system-software, time-sync) keep the **agent** case hard; so **Option A (segregating the learning agents) stands**, with separation-kernel partitioning at most a path for *deterministic* non-vital co-hosting. Design-basis update (E-2026-07-02-27 — Cloud4Rail safety-architecture comparison, DB InfraGO platform architects, 4/2026): the platform picture has since evolved — for existing trackside CCS the operator directionally chooses NON-certified COTS virtualisation with the safety argument anchored in a certified application-level safety layer plus Native Hardware Access, rather than a certified separation kernel (kept for onboard hard-real-time and GoA-4 cases). This reinforces rather than weakens Option A: the vital domain handles its own mixed-criticality via explicit certified safety layers, while common-cause failures and replication/voting remain open research in Cloud4Rail — nothing in this direction covers co-hosting learning components. An **in-operation** domestic precedent (E-2026-07-02-09 — DB InfraGO iLBS, 9/2024): the AnSi/EiSi procedures shifted safety responsibility out of the dispatcher operating level into the interlocking, making the HMI layer **non-SIL/COTS with simplified approval** while the vital layer enforces safety on every command — certified, running proof of the "non-vital layer over a safety-enforcing vital kernel" pattern and its approval/cost benefit; NB a *deterministic HMI* carrying *human-originated* commands, so it validates the human-command-path design, not agent co-hosting.

## Consequences

- **Easier:** R11 has a defined, certifiable boundary; ADR-003's classification gains its load-bearing design constraint; the safety case can argue independence by segregation.
- **Harder:** physical-segregation cost; the automation-bias obligation puts real weight on R10's human-oversight design; FFI / ISA / NoBo evidence must still be produced.
- **To revisit:** any autonomy increase (ADR-008 autonomy ladder) must be re-checked against this boundary before grant; Option B only if segregation proves impractical, and only with a partitioning case assessed to SIL-4.

**THE PROCEDURE TO FOLLOW, AND THE STANDARD TO OBTAIN (added 2026-08-19, E-2026-08-19-07 — Braband, ATO-RISK).** This ADR owes a system definition and a boundary specification (item 1); the DZSF-commissioned ATO-RISK project publishes a **CSM-compliant, semi-quantitative five-step procedure** that supplies both. **1. System definition** — identify in-scope functions, characterise each function's safety objective and framework conditions, determine **the objects protected by the function and affected by its failure**, and identify the relevant **operating scenarios**. **2. Hazard identification** per relevant failure type. **3. Select the risk acceptance principle** — **explicit risk analysis as a rule** where no code of practice or reference system exists, which is our position. **4. Determine severity** by accident category. **5. **⚠️ THE TECHNICAL BASIS FOR THIS ADR'S SEPARATION, STATED BY THE SAFETY AUTHORITY'S RESEARCH CENTRE (added 2026-08-19, E-2026-08-19-05).** Until now the decision to keep learning components outside the certified kernel has rested on an architectural judgement. DZSF states it as a technical impossibility: **for ML-based image-processing systems, "an analytical proof that excludes errors on UNKNOWN input parameters on the basis of KNOWN input parameters is NOT POSSIBLE"** — and therefore **"an autonomous train could systematically cause accidents as a result of undetected errors."** **The point is not that verification is difficult; it is that the analytical route is unavailable in principle for this class of component.** That is the strongest available support for the R11 separation and for the ADR-003 classification that depends on it, and it should be quoted in the EBA consultation. **Corollary for ADR-002 item 6:** if a learning component cannot be analytically cleared, the options are to keep it out of any safety-relevant decision class, or to pursue the **hybrid/explainable-AI** path DZSF is helping standardise (DIN SPEC 92001-3; ISO 21448 also proposes XAI for model trust) — and neither option is free.

**TRUSTWORTHINESS DOES NOT CLOSE AT ENTRY INTO SERVICE — AND THE OPERATOR HAS ALREADY CHOSEN THIS BOUNDARY TWICE (added 2026-08-20, E-2026-08-20-18, -15, -16).** **(1)** DLR's **InTra TP3** aims at methods assuring technical trustworthiness of highly automated rail systems *"für den vollständigen Lebenszyklus … während der Entwicklung, **aber auch während ihres Betriebes**"*, with verification approaches *"die als Grundlage für die Zulassung innovativer Bahnsysteme dienen können."* **A one-time pre-service safety case cannot discharge a component whose behaviour is learned or adaptive: the approval question becomes continuous. That is a second, independent ground for R11's separation — beside the impossibility-of-analytical-proof argument — and it is the stronger of the two operationally, because it does not depend on any claim about what analysis can achieve.** **(2) THE BOUNDARY IS ALREADY BEING DRAWN THIS WAY IN PRODUCTION, BY DB, TWICE.** *Schwelle 3.0* runs CNN crack detection across an estate of **~54 million concrete sleepers** with *Streckenverantwortliche* adjudicating every finding — and **the project page never mentions *Zulassung* or *Sicherheitsnachweis* at all.** *Digitale Weiche 2* runs AI fault detection on **650 operational switches** to 12/2027 with **TÜV Rheinland in the consortium from the start**, DB Netz aiming to establish predictive maintenance *"technisch und regulatorisch"*. **⚠️ Both are AI on safety-relevant infrastructure. Both detect and report; neither actuates; neither raises an authorisation question. This ADR's separation is not a theoretical construct — it is the arrangement the German infrastructure manager has independently chosen where it deploys AI, and the second case shows what taking an assessor along from the outset looks like, which is what item 1's ISA/NoBo engagement has been deferring.**

**A THIRD INDEPENDENT STATEMENT THAT COMPLETE ANALYTICAL DEMONSTRATION IS UNAVAILABLE — AND A REMOTE COMMAND PATH THAT CROSSES THIS BOUNDARY DIFFERENTLY (added 2026-08-20, E-2026-08-20-05, -07).** **(1)** DZSF's *ATO-Einsatzszenarien* states the premise for its own approval work: at GoA 3/4 the safety of the new technical systems **can, in principle, no longer be demonstrated by a complete analysis of how they function**. **With E-2026-08-19-05 (no analytical proof excluding errors on unknown inputs), E-2026-08-19-09 (scope-compliance uncertainty) and E-2026-08-19-11 (extrapolation extent not determinable), that is four formulations of one limit from four sources. R11's separation rests on firmer ground than this ADR has been claiming — state it as settled, in domain, rather than as our reading.** **(2)** ⚠️ **A remote command path is a different boundary problem from a local one.** *RemODtrAIn* (€40 m, Siemens Mobility coordinating, DB fleet, 10/2025–09/2028) is building a modular **safety architecture for remote train operation *nach CENELEC Prozess***, validated on an ICE 4, **for retrofit as well as new vehicles**. **Where command is exercised remotely, the human's authority traverses the bearer — so the boundary this ADR polices no longer separates only the learning layer from the vital layer; it must also account for a SAFETY-CARRYING COMMAND PATH that leaves the vehicle. Freedom from interference (item 1) has to be argued for that path too, and loss of it is not a degraded-comfort case but a loss of the commanding authority itself.** Note the AI scoping RemODtrAIn chose, which matches this ADR's own logic: **verifiable remote-control architecture on the safety-critical path, learned obstacle detection confined to depot, works and provisioning areas** — a bounded ODD with lower consequence.

**THE BOUNDARY ARGUMENT GENERALISES TO THE AI LAYER — AND IT IS THE SILENT-FAILOVER LESSON AGAIN (added 2026-08-19, E-2026-08-19-09 — DIN SPEC 92005 §7.5/§7.6).** The specification requires uncertainty quantification to follow **separation of concerns**: the ML model is responsible for producing accurate outputs, and **the uncertainty quantification is INDEPENDENTLY responsible for producing dependable uncertainty estimates** — and it **should be model-agnostic**, so that how uncertainty is judged does not depend on the model being judged. **That is this register's own detection principle, published as an AI requirement: a component that lies about its health cannot be its own alarm (PR5/PR11; ITU-T Q.752 out-of-band monitoring, E-2026-06-30-03). The 23-June failure was a component trusted to report its own state; an agent trusted to report its own confidence is the same architecture and the same failure mode one layer up.** For this ADR: **the confidence estimate that gates a HITL escalation must not be produced by the component whose output is being escalated.** ⚠️ **Attach the scope caveat: 92005 excludes safety functions and assistance systems from its requirements, so it supports the architectural argument and supplies none of the safety-case evidence — the FFI/independence analysis at item 1 is unaffected and still owed.**

**Assessment route and system-definition template (added 2026-08-19, E-2026-08-19-02 — DZSF *Standortbestimmung ATO*).** Two things this ADR needs and did not have. **(1) The applicable CSM-RA limb.** The register has been treating "CSM-RA" as one instrument; there are three distinct limbs in play — **Art 4(2) significance** (ADR-011), **§2.4 reference system** (E-2026-08-18-07, the route DZSF proposes for AI perception), and **§2.1.4 risk-acceptance principles**, under which **"in the absence of relevant codes of practice or technical reference systems, EXPLICIT risk estimation must be applied."** **That fallback is precisely our position: no code of practice and no technical reference system exists for an agentic oversight layer.** Under it, hazards are structured into failure scenarios, safety measures defined (§2.1.6), residual risk estimated and compared with a risk-acceptance criterion, and **the selected measures enter the system definition AS SAFETY REQUIREMENTS** — which is the mechanism by which this ADR's boundary becomes a requirement rather than an intention. **(2) A system-definition template.** CENELEC/CSM-RA require the assessment object to be fixed by defining the system's **purpose, boundaries and environmental conditions**, with a description claiming **completeness and delimitation**, and — regardless of how tasks are split between vehicle, infrastructure, LST and ATO — **the risks must ultimately be controlled.** DZSF's ATO Risk project built a **generic system definition at system level using DIN VDE V 0831-101 and DIN VDE V 0831-103**, characterising the system **largely independently of technical realisation**. **Obtain both standards — neither is in this register — and use that approach for the boundary specification at item 1.**

**Operating precedent for this boundary (added 2026-08-19, E-2026-08-19-01 — ERA/DZSF).** The separation this ADR proposes already exists in service on German railways and is worth citing as such: in electronic interlockings, **automatic route setting** performs both decision selection and action execution — yet **"due to safety standards, the train control system acts separately from the disposition system" and "usually has no access to current timetable and delay data."** Where delays cause conflicts, first-come-first-served is therefore not always appropriate and **manual disposition by the signaller is required**; in malfunctions the system may not continue automatically and the human takes over. **⚠️ AMENDED 2026-09-04 (E-2026-09-04-12) — CITE THIS PRECEDENT AS A BOUNDARY UNDER PRESSURE, NOT AS A SETTLED GIVEN.** DZSF Bericht 20 (2022), a technology forecast commissioned by the same research centre, contains use case §5.4 *"Steuernder Durchgriff der Disposition"* whose express purpose is to **remove** this isolation: AI-informed disposition, on a digital twin with HPC/quantum forecasting, transmitting **"Fahrempfehlungen & -kommandos"** — recommendations **and commands** — **immediately, as speed profiles and routes, to the vehicles, interlockings and RBCs**, over **FRMCS/satellite radio**. **The separation below is real and in service, but the sector's own research-funded roadmap contains a named proposal to dissolve it. This ADR must therefore ARGUE the boundary against that proposal rather than rest on the arrangement's existence — "it is how things are done" is not a defence against a roadmap item that exists precisely to change how things are done.** **Second consequence: it puts safety-carrying traffic on the bearer.** Where disposition issues commands over FRMCS, loss of the bearer stops being a degraded-advice case and becomes loss of commanding authority — the same escalation already recorded for remote operation (E-2026-08-20-07, RemODtrAIn). **Two independent sources now place command traffic on the FRMCS bearer, so "FRMCS carries advice only" is an UNVERIFIED ASSUMPTION of this register, not a property of the design (R3/R4).** **⚠️ SPLIT THE CONCERN IN TWO (2026-09-04, E-2026-09-04-16 — the attack graph for this very use case, Bericht 55 Abb. 49).** Tracing that graph: **FRMCS Jamming → *Kommunikation stören* → suppression of communication with interlocking and disposition → *Chaos schüren*** — an **AVAILABILITY** path ending in disruption and delay. **"Zugkollision herbeiführen" instead decomposes to *Zugposition manipulieren* / *Fahrempfehlungen manipulieren* / *Geschwindigkeitsprofil manipulieren*** — an **INTEGRITY** path, fed by *Kommunikation manipulieren*, *Lokalisierung manipulieren* and *KI manipulieren*. **The register has been arguing "the bearer carries safety traffic" as ONE concern. It is two, and they attach to different requirements: availability loss is R3/R4; integrity loss is R11 and this ADR's boundary. The command-path worry above is the INTEGRITY one — argue it as such, and stop letting the (better-evidenced, less severe) availability case carry it.** ⚠️ Bound: parent sets were traced, not exhaustively enumerated; no claim that jamming has no path to collision. **Bounds, corrected same day (E-2026-09-04-13):** ⚠️ **the forecast's horizon is 2050** — stated twice in its own Kurzfassung. **This is a DIRECTION OF TRAVEL, not an imminent programme, and this ADR must not argue as though a design were under way. What it must do is stop treating the separation as self-justifying, because a direction of travel is exactly what erodes an arrangement that is defended only by "this is how it is done."** Conversely the finding is **stronger** in one respect: the report states in both languages that **"the consideration of IT security is not yet part of this documentation but will be part of the following work packages"** — so §5.4 is published as a candidate future **with its security consideration expressly deferred**, not merely unassessed in what we hold. Teil 2 is a firm acquisition target. Also: a 2022 forecast, plan-not-outcome, and only §5.4 of its 21 use cases was read.

**Two consequences for this engagement: (1) the safety-carrying system being deliberately isolated from optimisation data is an ESTABLISHED arrangement, not a novel constraint we are imposing; (2) that isolation itself creates an information gap which only the human currently spans — so an advisory layer that reads disposition data and informs the signaller fills that gap WITHOUT crossing the boundary.
