## Resolution 1 — Q-2 (ADR-012): **carried in part, and split**

Carried.** Item 9b's permanent accepted residual is **not lifted and is not weakened**: the normative scales, severity tables and threat-scoring content remain unheld, and the engagement-defined attacker scales continue to claim no derivation from −104. ## Resolution 2 — Q-3 (ADR-002): **carried as to the guardrail; the model-poisoning placement is REJECTED and re-routed**

**2a — the guardrail's rationale becomes dual-basis. Carried.** ADR-002's *agents advise, never actuate* has rested on safety reasoning — EN 50129 composition, the impossibility of analytical proof for learning components, human-in-command. **BSI (E-2026-09-04-17) independently identifies the same boundary as the security-critical property**, naming prompt injection and indirect prompt injection as critical **"above all in agentic systems … where their outputs can trigger direct actions"**, and prescribing isolation, validation layers and permission concepts. **The guardrail is henceforth argued on two independent bases, from two disciplines, by two authorities that have never spoken to this engagement. The board regards this as the single strongest external support any position in this register holds — and records, in the same breath, that it is corroboration of a decision already made, not permission to widen it.**

**2b — ⚠️ the proposal to fold model poisoning into ADR-002 item 6 is REJECTED, on two grounds.** **First, item 6 is closed** — decided 2026-08-20, implementation class bound to decision class. Reopening a closed item to carry an unrelated threat would corrupt the record of what was decided. **Second, and more important, the placement is simply wrong: model poisoning is not a verifiability question.** It is a **supply-chain integrity control**, and it belongs where such controls live.

**2c — what the board records instead, and it is sharper than the proposal.** ⚠️ **The guardrail does not defend against a poisoned model. It bounds the blast radius and does not prevent the harm.** An agent that never actuates can still, with a substituted or poisoned model, emit confident wrong advice that a human acts on — and ADR-004's own automation-bias work exists precisely because humans act on advice. **"Advise, never actuate" limits what an agent can do directly; it does nothing about what it can persuade a person to do. That distinction is now stated in ADR-002 so the guardrail is not read as a general answer to model integrity.** Routing: the control goes to **ADR-012's procurement gate** (provenance, trusted sources, signed models, integrity verification), and the test class is already written into **ADR-007 surface 6** (E-2026-09-04-17). Two independent sources support it — BSI, and the DZSF/INCYDE attack-graph leaf at E-2026-09-04-16.

## Resolution 3 — Q-4 (tempo): **carried as amended — the board rejects the framing of "which regime wins" and substitutes a population split**

**3a — the conflict is real and is stated.** BSI (22.06.2026): triage-to-rollout in **minutes to at most a few hours**, and **"a few days is not an adequate response speed"**; exploits from patch-diffing within hours; Mandiant's observed **median time-to-exploitation of minus seven days**. Against: much of the rail estate **frequently cannot take an update at all** (E-2026-09-04-14); Class-A changes carry CSM-RA significance assessment and AsBo sign-off (ADR-011); CRA Art 14(2)(c)'s 14-day clock is itself slower than the advisory's tempo.

**3b — ⚠️ but the board declines to adjudicate it as a contest between regimes, because that framing is wrong.** The advisory addresses **IT estates**; ADR-011's change control governs **safety-certified OT**. **These are not competing claims over one population — they are correct claims over two different populations, and the register has been treating its estate as one thing.** The resolution is therefore **not** a winner. It is a **split**, and the instrument for it already exists: **ADR-012 item 1's PDE inventory, including its "undefended systems" rule (E-2026-08-20-32) — passenger information, fleet management, administrative systems.**

**3c — the split, as decided:**

1. **Vital and safety-certified systems** — ADR-011 governs without exception. **BSI tempo is not imported here, and no security argument may be used to shorten a CSM-RA assessment or bypass an AsBo.** The residual risk of a slow patch on a vital system is accepted deliberately, and is the price of the boundary.
2. **IT-side and undefended systems** — the oversight layer's own infrastructure, monitoring, dispatcher workstations, information and administrative systems. **Here the BSI tempo applies and the register has no excuse: these are ordinary IT estates that happen to sit near a railway.** ADR-012 item 1's inventory must mark each PDE with which population it belongs to.
3. **The boundary between them is itself a control**, and is ADR-004's boundary seen from the security side: if an IT-side compromise can reach the vital side, the split is fictional and the whole argument fails.

**3d — PR16 is re-characterised.** From *"security-update change burden"* to: **a structural conflict between two regimes binding one operator, resolved by population split, with a named accepted residual on the vital side and no residual on the IT side.** **The board notes that a risk recorded as a "burden" invites the answer "work harder", which is not available here — part of the estate cannot be patched at any tempo.**

**3e — carried into the procurement gate (the Q-2 half merged at Resolution 1c):** every PDE is asked, before purchase, **whether it can take a security update at all, by what mechanism, within what window, and for how long the vendor will supply them.** A PDE that cannot be updated is not disqualified — but it is a **deliberate, recorded acceptance**, not a discovery made after installation. **Also carried: CVSS base score alone is insufficient where AI can chain vulnerabilities; supplement with environment-dependent metrics (e.g. CVSS 4.0 Environmental).**
