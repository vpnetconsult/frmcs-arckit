## Resolution 2 — **option (b) is carried**: remote command is in scope, on stated terms

Option (a) — define the class-4 human as locally present and put teleoperation out of scope — **is rejected**. The sector has funded (b): teleoperation is the *designed* ATO fallback level (E-2026-08-20-02), and RemODtrAIn is a €40 m programme building a CENELEC-process safety architecture for remote train operation on an ICE 4 (E-2026-08-20-05). **Declaring out of scope the thing the deployment context is being built around would make this ADR inapplicable to the railway it describes.**

⚠️ **Scope guard, which Q-5 did not propose and the board adds.** This ADR does **not** design teleoperation and holds no view on how it should be built. What it decides is narrower and is the only thing properly ours: **the preconditions our oversight layer's HITL ladder requires when the accountable human may be remote.** The ladder had silently assumed presence; that assumption is now stated and conditioned.

**The amendment to the ladder is carried as drafted at Resolution 3.**

## Resolution 3 — the ladder amendment, as written into ADR-002

**Class 4 (safety-critical actuation) — human-in-command** gains:

1. **A presence declaration.** Every class-4 arrangement must state whether the commanding human is **locally present** or **remote**. There is no default, and silence is not "local".
2. **A bearer-availability precondition where remote.** Where command is exercised remotely, class-4 authority is **conditional on the command path**, and the arrangement must state the availability property it depends on and how loss is *detected* — not merely that redundancy exists. **This is the 23-June lesson applied to authority rather than to traffic: the failure there was undetected silence, not absent redundancy (E-2026-06-27-01).**
3. **A defined loss-of-command behaviour.** What the system does when the command path is lost must be **specified as a safety property, not left to degrade**. ⚠️ **The register's founding incident gets worse under remote command, not better: in June 2026 trains stopped safely because a human was ON BOARD to receive a written *Befehl*. Move that human to a control centre and the same silence removes the recovery path as well as the supervision path.**
4. **The escalation-of-regime rule.** Advice and control are **different regimes, not different modes** — road law already separates *Teleassistenz* (recommendations only, availability of a *Technische Aufsicht* a precondition of operation) from *Telefahren* (direct remote control) (E-2026-08-20-20). **Any movement of our layer from advising a remote human toward acting for one is a regime change and returns to this board.** Cross-domain caution applies: the structure transfers, the instruments do not.

⚠️ **Bound on the whole amendment, carried from Q-5 point 4 and binding on how it may be cited:** the exposure is **real but not uniform.** The sector's own design retains **"Zugbegleiter-Plus"** on-board staff who resolve situations together with the Tele-Tf, with unattended continuation only in an assumed **"best case"** (E-2026-09-04-18). **The bearer-dependency risk bites hardest exactly in that unattended best case — which the project itself treats as aspirational — and not in the staffed scenarios it treats as normal. Do not cite this amendment as though every teleoperated train were an unattended one.**

## Resolution 5 — the governance defect, and a rule adopted

⚠️ **A decision-shaped item was gated on attending an event, with no named owner, no calendar trigger, and no defined behaviour if the date passed. It passed. Three days elapsed before the register noticed, and only because the item was chased for other reasons.**

**Rule adopted, applying to every future item gated on an external event:** the item must name **(i) the event and its date, (ii) an owner responsible for attending or obtaining the output, and (iii) what happens if the date passes with the output not obtained.** **An item that cannot state (iii) is not gated — it is merely postponed, and must not be written as though a mechanism existed.**
