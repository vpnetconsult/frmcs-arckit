## Context

ADR-001 commits to FRMCS (5G SA + MCX) as a resilient, bearer-flexible transport. It does not, by itself, govern *decisions* — neither decisions about the architecture (the ADR, the RFP evaluation, the risk register) nor decisions within the running network (fault detection, fail-soft, bearer fallback). Today both are episodic, human, and slow: the 23–24 June 2026 DB GSM-R outage (incident-annex.md) was a roughly two-hour standstill in which a known, recurring risk had no agent watching it and no system authorised to act on it. That is a decision-and-governance gap, not only a radio gap.

A critical clarification carried from ADR-001: **5G/FRMCS is an enabler of autonomy, not autonomy itself.** The bearer provides the bandwidth, latency, and mission-critical services that make ATO and agentic decisions possible; autonomy is a separate layer riding on top, with its own governance. Conflating the two is how a programme buys "5G" and assumes resilience and autonomy arrived in the box.

## Decision

Introduce an **agentic decision & oversight layer** over the FRMCS bearer, split into two planes, with human oversight bounded by the reversibility and safety-impact of each action and a named human accountable for every decision class. Keep the SIL-4 safety kernel deterministic and outside the learning agents.

### Two planes

Decision plane — governs the architecture:
- `Risk Sentinel` — continuously ingests obsolescence signals, incident telemetry, supplier/market and spectrum/regulatory changes; maintains a live risk register; raises a decision-ready alert before a threshold is crossed. (Closes the "why nobody knew" gap — R12.)
- `Architecture Decision Agent` — re-evaluates ADR options when inputs move; drafts the delta; routes to the ARB.
- `Bid/Procurement Agent` — scores RFP responses against weighted criteria; flags vendor concentration/lock-in; attaches source-trust tiers to ingested claims. (R8.)
- `Assurance Agent` — maps each decision to CCS TSI, CENELEC, NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act; emits the audit/evidence chain.

Runtime plane — governs the live network:
- `Anomaly/Fault Agent` — detects the central-SPOF signature (nationwide-simultaneous ≠ RF) faster than a human duty manager. (R3.)
- `Resilience Orchestration Agent` — proposes fail-soft/degraded mode and multi-bearer fallback; does not actuate safety-critical state autonomously. (R4.)
- `Intent Agents (BIA/SIA/RIA)` — compile operational intent to CAMARA/network actions for reversible, non-safety actions.

### Human-in-the-loop model (oversight by decision class)

| Decision class | Example | Autonomy | Human role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reversible, no safety impact | Re-score an RFP claim; refresh risk register | Autonomous | On-the-loop (audit) |
| Reversible, operational | Draft ADR delta; propose capacity slice | Human-on-the-loop | Can intervene; periodic review |
| Irreversible / financial | Award recommendation; fallback to public bearer | Human-in-the-loop | Approves before action |
| Safety-critical actuation | Movement authority; emergency stop; degraded-mode entry | Human-in-command | Human initiates; agent advises only — never autonomous. **⚠️ Presence is no longer assumed — see the class-4 remote-command preconditions below** |

**The ladder in the bearer's own management vocabulary (E-2026-09-19-06 — 3GPP TS 28.312, read 2026-09-19).** 3GPP's intent lifecycle is RECEIVED → EVALUATING → FULFILLING → FULFILLED/DEGRADED, with **no human role and no approval state**: consumer and producer are roles (CSC/CSP/NOP) that may both be software. The one phase the spec guarantees changes nothing in the network is **pre-evaluation** — feasibility check and intent exploration. **That is where this ladder attaches:** for classes 3–4 the oversight layer acts as intent *consumer* and holds the intent in EVALUATING until the human has advised the preferred outcome (28.312 §4.6.3 *"advising on preferred outcome"*); an intent is *created* for fulfilment only on that act. Class 1–2 intents may proceed to FULFILLING on evidence thresholds (item 5). **Two consequences: (i) a class-4 action is never expressed as an intent at all — 28.312 has no primitive that keeps a human in command *during* fulfilment, so safety-critical actuation stays outside the intent system entirely, as the guardrail already requires; (ii) an intent whose producer may resolve conflicts by *"suspension of the whole intent"* (§5.3.4.3) is not a fit vehicle for a class-A–C service unless suspension is excluded by explicit priority guidance (§5.3.4.4 CON-4) — see ADR-001 item 5(b).** **The assurance-loop companion confirms it from the other side (E-2026-09-19-11 — 3GPP TS 28.535 V19.0.0, read 2026-09-19).** 28.535 defines the *closed* loop as one with no human inside it — goals and policies in, status out — and the *open* loop as one where a human intervenes in a step. Its §6.2 then lists twenty-two requirements: nineteen let the loop act (ascertain breach, root-cause, "take corrective actions", reallocate resources on prediction), and **exactly three let the consumer constrain it — limit the executable action set (REQ-CSA-CON-16), gate the loop on a condition (CON-17), pause before an action executes (CON-20) — and each of the three is marked "not supported".** **Consequence for this ladder: a class-3 hold cannot be delegated to the bearer's assurance loop in Rel-19, because the loop has no supported primitive for it. The hold is implemented above the loop — the oversight layer is the loop's consumer and withholds the *goal* (28.535 §4.2.4: the human "provides control outside the loop"), never relying on a pause inside it — or the action is not handed to a closed loop at all (class 4).** 28.535's own escalation (loop "cannot automatically adjust" → human "needs to be informed" → human "may decide" to make it open) is informed-after-the-fact, the 23-June shape; PR11 carries it. **Verified against Stage 2/3 (E-2026-09-19-12 — TS 28.536 V19.2.0 + its OpenAPI fragment, read 2026-09-19): the `AssuranceClosedControlLoop` IOC gives the consumer exactly three controls — `administrativeState` (LOCKED by default; the consumer unlocks), `aCCLDisallowedList` (named attributes of named managed objects the loop may not modify), and the goal. No pause point exists; the SLS assurance procedure changes resources at step 9 and confirms fulfilment to the consumer at step 11. So the ladder's use of the bearer is settled: class-3/4 objects go on the deny list (the wire form of ADR-011's vital-object exclusion), a loop stays LOCKED until the oversight layer unlocks it with a goal, and the human hold is never expected from inside the loop — because there is nothing inside it to hold.**

**⚠️ CLASS-4 PRECONDITIONS WHERE THE COMMANDING HUMAN MAY BE REMOTE (amended ARB-2026-09-04/4 R2/R3, closing item 8; E-2026-09-04-18, E-2026-08-20-01/-02/-05/-20).** This ladder assumed — without ever stating it — that the accountable human is **present**. The sector has funded the opposite: teleoperation is the *designed* ATO fallback level, and remote train operation is a €40 m programme building a CENELEC-process safety architecture on an ICE 4. **The assumption is therefore stated and conditioned rather than left silent.** Four preconditions attach to any class-4 arrangement:

1. **Presence declaration.** State whether the commanding human is **locally present** or **remote**. There is no default, and **silence is not "local"**.
2. **Bearer-availability precondition where remote.** Remote class-4 authority is **conditional on the command path**. The arrangement must state the availability property it depends on **and how loss of that path is DETECTED** — not merely that redundancy exists. **This is the 23-June lesson applied to authority rather than to traffic: that failure was undetected silence, not absent redundancy (E-2026-06-27-01).**
3. **Defined loss-of-command behaviour**, specified as a safety property rather than left to degrade. ⚠️ **The founding incident gets WORSE under remote command, not better: in June 2026 trains stopped safely because a human was ON BOARD to receive a written *Befehl*. Move that human to a control centre and the same silence removes the recovery path as well as the supervision path.**
4. **Escalation of regime returns to the board.** Advice and control are **different regimes, not different modes** — road law already separates *Teleassistenz* (recommendations only, with availability of a *Technische Aufsicht* a precondition of operation) from *Telefahren* (direct remote control). **Any movement of this layer from advising a remote human toward acting for one is a regime change, not a mode change.** The structure transfers from road; the instruments do not.

**Scope guard:** this ADR does **not** design teleoperation and holds no view on how it should be built. It specifies only what **our oversight layer's** ladder requires when the accountable human may be remote.

⚠️ **Bound on citation, binding:** the exposure is **real but not uniform.** The sector's design retains **"Zugbegleiter-Plus"** on-board staff who resolve situations together with the Tele-Tf, with unattended continuation only in an assumed **"best case"**. **The bearer dependency bites hardest in that unattended best case — which the project itself treats as aspirational — not in the staffed scenarios it treats as normal. Do not cite this as though every teleoperated train were unattended.**

### Non-negotiables

- The **SIL-4 safety kernel stays deterministic and outside the learning agent** (CENELEC EN 50126/28/29). The agent optimises and advises around a certified safety core; it does not become the safety core. (R11.)
- **Automation bias** is a designed-against failure: a human rubber-stamping agent proposals is not oversight. The model must make dissent cheap and the rationale legible.
- **Accountability does not transfer.** The agent never owns the decision; a person does.

### Implementation class — what the agents may be built from (decided 2026-08-20)

**The rule: implementation class is bound to decision class.** The HITL ladder already says what an agent may *do*; this says what it may *be*, on the same axis. Nothing else in this ADR changes.

**1. Safety-relevant classes — fully verifiable only.** Any component that produces or **gates** an output reaching decision class 3 (irreversible / financial) or class 4 (safety-critical actuation) must be **fully verifiable** in the DZSF sense (E-2026-08-19-02): **decision rules traceable, or behaviour verifiable as correct across the input space.** Learned components — expressly including deep neural networks — **may not** produce or gate those outputs. This is option (a) of the two the item posed, and the capability ceiling it imposes is accepted deliberately.

**2. Reversible classes — learned components permitted, under conditions.** For classes 1 and 2 a learned component is allowed, provided **all** of the following are recorded before it goes live:
- **the three-part transparency score** — algorithmic transparency · decomposability · simulatability (DIN SPEC 92005 §3.25), scored and recorded, not asserted. A white-box model is not automatically transparent;
- **stakeholder-relative explainability demonstrated for the actual human at the actual moment** — explainable *to the duty manager, at the alert, sufficiently to act on*, not explainable to a developer in validation (DIN SPEC 92001-3);
- **uncertainty quantification produced independently of the model being judged**, and model-agnostic (DIN SPEC 92005 §7.5/§7.6) — the component may not be its own confidence estimator;
- **calibration evidenced and overconfidence excluded** (§7.8/§7.9), on a dedicated calibration dataset;
- **the explanation itself treated as failure-capable and measured** in both directions — expert overrides a correct recommendation, operator over-trusts a wrong one (DIN SPEC 92001-3 §6.6). Explanation is not the mitigation for automation bias; it is another thing to measure;
- **model provenance evidence on record (sixth condition, added ARB-2026-09-17 R2 by cross-reference to ADR-012 Decision item 1, closing Q-18):** model identity and version · training-data provenance and licence · evaluation record against the declared ODD (ADR-010 item 5) · weight-update mechanism and window · CVD contact for model vulnerabilities — format unspecified. Distinct from rule 2a, which asks whether training was *lawful*; this asks what the component *is* and how it will change.

**2a. Data-protection precondition for generative components (added 2026-08-27, E-2026-08-27-01).** The five conditions above govern a learned component's *oversight quality*, not its *lawfulness*. Where a class-1/2 learned component is a **generative AI model** (e.g. an LLM-based drafting/summarising agent), the Dutch DPA's June-2026 guidance — operationalising EDPB Opinion 28/2024 — holds that a model trained on personal data is **not inherently anonymous**, so the deploying controller carries GDPR duties **before the component goes live**: a prior lawfulness-of-training assessment (duty of investigation), contractual arrangements on the trained model's lawfulness, developer-supplied evidence (per-dataset lawfulness assessment, training-procedure information, LIA), verification of the developer's AI Act GPAI / Code-of-Practice compliance, and DPIA results — plus output filters against regurgitation of personal data (state of the art short-term; longer term, a model no longer containing the data). **These are procurement-time preconditions of the same rank as the five above: no generative component enters a class until this evidence exists** — same discipline as ADR-010 items 6–8. Bounds: NL authority (persuasive, EDPB-anchored, not binding in DE); guidance self-declared valid only until EDPB genAI guidelines or the GDPR-amending Digital Omnibus (distinct from Reg (EU) 2026/1744 — see E-2026-08-27-01e).

**3. Hybrid is the preferred shape where coverage matters: a learned component may PROPOSE, a verifiable component must GATE.** Where a learned component earns its place by detecting cases a rule set misses — the superset finding at E-2026-08-19-12 — it may run, but **the thing that decides is the verifiable component.** This is the "advise, never actuate" guardrail applied one level down, inside the layer rather than at its edge, and it is the pattern DZSF is helping standardise (hybrid / explainable AI, DIN SPEC 92001-3).

**The sector's mirror of rules 2–4 (E-2026-09-19-08, 2026-09-19): 3GPP TS 28.105 specifies the ML model lifecycle as management services — training · testing · *inference emulation before deployment* (§6.3) · deployment with model registration · inference with per-inference *history and context* (§6.5.5), *explainability reported to the consumer* (§6.5.7), consumer-triggered update on decay (§6.5.2), and *reversion to non-ML operation without service interruption* for underperforming models (§6.5.6). Every one of those maps onto a rule below or onto ADR-010; what 28.105 does not have is a human at any step — its consumer is a role that may be software. The rules below are the deployment choice 28.105 leaves to the operator, made.** **Third instance of the pattern (E-2026-09-19-11, TS 28.535 V19.0.0): the assurance closed-loop spec names the consumer-side constraints rule 2's hold would use — bounded action set, conditional enable, pre-execution pause (REQ-CSA-CON-16/-17/-20) — and marks all three "not supported". 28.312 has no approval state, 28.105 has no human step, 28.535 has the hold as text without a mechanism: the rules below are not a duplication of what the bearer's management plane provides, they are the part it does not.**

**4. Where both candidates meet the coverage requirement, prefer the one whose failures are CORRECTABLE over the one whose failure rate is merely TUNABLE.** Rule-based errors are configuration faults or model incompleteness and are fixed by amending the model; learned-component error rates are traded against each other and never eliminated (E-2026-08-19-12, ch. 7). **For a layer whose entire claim is that it can be governed, correctability is the property that claim rests on.**

**⚠️ 5. Anti-laundering clause — the class of an output is set by the highest decision class it materially informs, not by the agent that emitted it.** A learned component cannot be placed in class 1, have its output read by a human, and thereby feed a class-4 decision as though the constraint had been satisfied. **If an output materially informs a safety-critical decision, it is a safety-relevant output and rule 1 applies to whatever produced it.** Without this clause the rest is decorative: every safety decision in this architecture is taken by a human reading agent output, so class could otherwise always be argued downward.

**What this gives up, stated plainly.** Constraining the safety-relevant path to verifiable methods **costs coverage**. The in-domain evidence is explicit that a learned detector finds a superset of what a rule set finds, including infrequent cases and local subtleties nobody enumerated. **We are choosing narrower, correctable, provable coverage over broader, tuned, unprovable coverage — and accepting that some detectable condition will be missed that a learned component would have caught.** That is a real cost and it is not offset by rule 3; hybrid recovers some of it in the proposing role only.

**What this does not decide.** It does not decide *which* verifiable technique (rule set, decision tree, formal model). It does not license any learned component by itself — rule 2's conditions are preconditions, not a checklist to be waved through. And it does not touch ADR-004's boundary, which polices where the layer sits relative to the certified kernel; **this rule operates entirely inside the advisory layer and assumes that boundary holds.**

**Evidence conditions.** ADR-010 carries the measurement: the transparency score, the calibration and overconfidence evidence, and the two-directional explanation-failure metrics are eval-harness outputs, not design assertions. **No component enters a class until its evidence exists** (ADR-010 items 6, 7, 8).

## Options considered

### Option A — No agentic layer (status quo)
Episodic human decisions, reactive incident response.
**Pros:** nothing new to assure. **Cons:** reproduces the 23–24 Jun failure mode — known risk unwatched, no authorised fast action. Rejected as the target.

### Option B — Agentic layer, advisory-only across all classes
Agents propose; humans execute everything.
**Pros:** simplest assurance; no autonomous action. **Cons:** loses the speed benefit where it is safe and valuable (audit/operational classes); humans remain the bottleneck for reversible actions. Partial.

### Option C — Agentic layer with oversight bounded by decision class (recommended)
Autonomy where reversible and safe; human-in-the-loop where irreversible/financial; human-in-command for safety actuation.
**Pros:** speed where safe, control where it matters; certifiable core preserved; closes the awareness gap. **Cons:** requires a real AI management system (NIST AI RMF / ISO 42001) and eval/drift monitoring. ~~and an EU AI Act high-risk compliance posture~~ — **CORRECTED 2026-08-15: this ADR asserted a high-risk compliance posture as a settled cost. It is not. ADR-003 verified against primary law (Reg (EU) 2024/1689) on 2026-06-24 — the same day this ADR was written — that the layer AS ARCHITECTED HERE (oversight not control, non-actuating, human-in-command) sits OUTSIDE the high-risk perimeter: not high-risk as designed, conditional on the Art 3(14) safety-component test. See ADR-003 §Verification findings; evidence E-2026-06-24-07 (tier A).** Recommended.

## Trade-off analysis

The dominant trade is **speed of proposal vs autonomy of action**. The agent's value is fast, well-evidenced *proposals*, not autonomous safety action. Bounding autonomy by decision class captures the speed (replaying the outage: anomaly flagged in seconds, fallback proposed with predicted impact, duty manager approves a bounded action in one click) while the safety-critical halt/restart stays human-in-command. The two-hour standstill becomes a supervised, minutes-long degraded mode.

**The counter-position, recorded at its strongest (2026-09-15, E-2026-09-15-03).** Consumer telecom's stated direction for TM Forum Autonomous Networks Level 4 is closed-loop self-management, and a tier-1 operator's architect puts it plainly: *"We have to remove the human from the loop otherwise the network will get so complex that it is unmanageable by humans"* (Telstra, in a vendor-sponsored TM Forum report). This ADR holds the opposite for decision classes 3–4 and R9 exists because autonomy over a rail bearer must be a governable layer, not an inherent property of the network. The objection is legitimate — complexity is real and human bandwidth is bounded (PR4) — and the answer is the ladder, not a denial: automate what is reversible, bound the human's load, keep command human where the consequence is irreversible. The same source's own stack still places humans at the top "in the loop as needed".
