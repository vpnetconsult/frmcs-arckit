## Resolution 1 — the R4 breach: **regularised, recorded as a THIRD occurrence, and the rule is tightened**

⚠️ **The facts, disclosed by the assistant in the paper rather than found by audit.** ARB-2026-09-04 R4 adopted a standing instruction that an edit to an ADR whose Status is `Accepted` is drafted as a proposed amendment and **comes to the board before it is folded into the ADR body**. On 2026-09-05 the assistant amended **ADR-011 and ADR-012 — both `Accepted` — directly, six times, queueing none of them.** ADR-004 and ADR-007 are `Proposed`; those edits were legitimate and are not in issue.

**1a — the six amendments are REGULARISED**, on their merits, at Resolutions 2 and 3. The board notes it is not approving proposals but **ratifying text already in the file**, which is a materially weaker position and is recorded as such.

**1b — this is the THIRD occurrence.** Recorded at ARB-2026-08-23 R1 §4; recorded again as a repeat at ARB-2026-09-04 R4, which adopted the rule; breached again the next working day. **The board draws the conclusion the pattern supports: a rule that depends on the actor interrupting their own workflow to comply has now failed three times, and the failure is in the rule's design, not only in its observance.**

**1c — R4 IS TIGHTENED. The queue becomes the ONLY write path to an `Accepted` ADR.** From this minute: an amendment to a ratified decision is **written first as a Q-entry in `06-ratification-readiness.md`**, and the ADR body is edited **only** by a board act citing that entry. **The point is enforceability by visibility: a Q-entry that exists with no matching minute is a defect anyone can see in the file, whereas an unqueued edit is invisible until someone audits the diff.** The rule now fails loudly instead of silently.

**1d — the board declines to treat this as settled by rule-writing alone.** ⚠️ **If a fourth breach occurs, the honest response is not a fourth rule but a finding that the register's amendment discipline does not survive its own working practice — and that belongs in the decision-drift write-up, not in another instruction.**

## Resolution 2 — ADR-011 (`Accepted`): the legacy redefinition. **Carried.**

**2a — "legacy" is capability-based, not age-based.** Per RSEG 25E157-1A §3.2 (E-2026-09-05-07), legacy describes systems lacking the cybersecurity capabilities required from today's perspective **regardless of how long they have been in operation**, expressly including systems **newly delivered and/or installed** that were not built to **EULYNX BL4R2+, ERJU SP Cybersecurity Requirements, CLC/TS 50701 or EN IEC 62443**. *"Only systems developed according to the applicable and relevant regulations are treated as non-legacy."*

⚠️ **The consequence the board adopts, and it changes what this ADR believes it governs: ADR-011 has treated legacy as the OLD estate the migration moves away from. On the sector's definition a new FRMCS component procured without conformance is LEGACY ON THE DAY IT IS INSTALLED. The migration does not retire the legacy problem; it can MANUFACTURE it. What decides which is ADR-012 item 2's procurement gate — so that gate is load-bearing for ADR-011, not adjacent to it.**

**2b — a security overlay is a change to the availability budget.** Per RSEG 24E122-1A §2.3 (four mandatory clauses): on any VPN mitigation the railway **shall reassess** network availability including the VPN components, and **shall ensure** required availability, **latency tolerance** and **bandwidth** are maintained; segmentation is preserved by construction — the VPN **shall provide the same segmentation as the existing installation**. **Carried into the pre-change gate: a VPN retrofit is exactly the "security improvement" that can degrade the availability R3/R4 exist to protect, and the budget is to be RE-MEASURED, not assumed.**
