## Draft enquiry

**Subject:** Rail operational communications — questions on SOC information flows, TR-03183, and CRA/RED overlap

Dear ACS team,

We work on architecture and governance for railway operational communications — specifically the GSM-R → FRMCS (5G) transition and the cybersecurity conformance that comes with it. We have joined the Allianz für Cyber-Sicherheit and would value guidance, or a pointer to the right working group, on four questions.

**1. National SOC information flows — can a rail infrastructure operator be a recipient rather than a builder?**

The most useful thing we could learn. European rail operators have been measured as **strong at logging but weak at detection and at log correlation and analysis** — the capability gap, not the data gap. We also understand the EU's Digital Europe Programme has funded **national and cross-border SOC platforms** intended to deliver early warnings to critical-infrastructure operators.

An operator we have studied left exactly this question open in its own on-train security programme: *"do we need an OT SOC or not?"*

- Is there an established route by which a rail infrastructure operator in Germany **receives** early warnings and threat intelligence from national SOC/CERT structures, rather than building full detection capability in-house?
- Where does the ACS itself sit in that flow, and what does membership actually give us operationally?
- Is there existing ACS or BSI material on **OT/ICS detection for rail**, as distinct from generic IT SOC guidance?

**2. BSI TR-03183 — currency and applicability**

TR-03183 sits in our cybersecurity conformance decision as a sector interface, largely for the CRA-related product requirements (SBOM, vulnerability handling, support periods).

- Is the current version the one we should be building procurement requirements against, and is a revision expected ahead of the CRA becoming fully applicable?
- Is it intended to apply to **long-life, safety-adjacent products with digital elements** — cab radios, on-board gateways, trackside equipment with 20-year-plus service lives — or is it aimed at shorter-lifecycle IT products?

**3. CRA and the Radio Equipment Directive — which governs, and when?**

This is a genuine open question for us. FRMCS equipment is **radio equipment**, so Directive 2014/53/EU applies — we have confirmed from the directive text that rail has no sectoral exemption, unlike marine and aviation. Its Article 3(3) cybersecurity requirements are activated per equipment class by delegated act. The same products are also "products with digital elements" under the CRA.

- **Do the two regimes overlap for this equipment class, and does one displace the other on a date?** If so, that date is a programme milestone for us.
- Is there BSI guidance on how manufacturers and operators should sequence the two?

**4. Security updates versus authorisation stability**

Our load-bearing tension: the CRA requires security updates across a declared support period, while railway authorisation favours stability, and every update to an authorised safety-carrying product is a change that must pass change control.

- Is there BSI or ACS material on this in regulated or safety-critical sectors — particularly on an **expedited but still governed** path for actively exploited vulnerabilities, rather than an emergency bypass?

We are happy to contribute back. The transition raises questions — safety-critical OT with a long asset life, a multi-year dual-run, an unusually constrained patching path — that may be of interest to others in the network.

With thanks,
[name], Vpnet Cloud Solutions Sdn. Bhd.
