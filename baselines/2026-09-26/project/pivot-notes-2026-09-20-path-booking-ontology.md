# Pivot notes — path booking across several infrastructure managers: the entities, in ontology terms

**Date:** 2026-09-20 · **Pivot state:** `current/` at 530 rows (`E-2026-09-19-50` last); baseline `2026-09-19_2321`. · **Siblings:** `pivot-notes-2026-09-19-ontology.md` (the layers: ontology and law), `13-legal-binding-chain.md`, `sdo-mapping-frmcs-gsmr-5gsa.md`.
**Purpose:** name every party and object in a cross-border train path — who asks, who allocates, who runs, who owns the wagons, who carries the radio — and say for each whether a governed vocabulary term exists, which one, and where the public mobile operator does and does not enter. This is the register's map for the corridor declaration of ADR-001 item 5(c) and for the "what rides on the bearer" federation of ADR-002 item 9.

**Outcome anchor:** *safe, continuous rail operations.* A path is a promise between organisations that a train may occupy a section of line at a time. When the bearer under that train fails at a border, the question "whose network, whose rule, whose train" has to be answerable in the same terms on both sides.

**Guardrail:** oversight, not control. Nothing here books a path; it records who does and in which words.

**Method note:** "ontology term" means an IRI in a governed vocabulary (`era:`, `era-organisation-roles:`, the telematics classes of the ERA Ontology, `vpa:`, the ISS scheme, the TM Forum TIO/SID). Legal instruments the register does not hold are named as such (tier: statute known, text not held — B10 lineage).

---

## 1. The entities

The path-booking world has three planes: the **contractual plane** (who may ask, who must answer — Directive 2012/34/EU and the freight-corridor regulation), the **message plane** (the Telematics TSI — Reg (EU) 2026/253 with TD100/TD105, now lifted into the ERA Ontology as telematics classes), and the **register plane** (RINF, ERATV, EVR, ERADIS — the ERA Ontology proper). The bearer is a fourth plane that the first three assume and never name.

## 2. The flow, in the held vocabulary

TD100's path lifecycle, written in the ERA telematics classes (each arrow is a message class; each party is a `companyCodeType`):

```
Freight customer ──ConsignmentOrderMessage──▶ Lead RU ──ConsignmentOrderMessage──▶ RU_1 … RU_n
RU_i ──PathRequestMessage──▶ IM_i            (one request per IM involved; TrainInformation covers the whole journey)
IM_i ──PathDetailsMessage / PathNotAvailableMessage──▶ RU_i
RU_i ──PathConfirmedMessage | PathDetailsRefusedMessage──▶ IM_i
RU_i ──TrainCompositionMessage──▶ IM_i       (vehicles → RCC compatibility on IM_i's RINF sections)
RU_i ──TrainReadyMessage (trainContactDetails)──▶ IM_i
IM_i ──TrainForecastAtReportingLocationMessage / TrainLocationReport──▶ RU_i, RU_{i+1}
at ReferenceBorderPoint: OperationalTrainNumberIdentifier.scheduledTimeAtHandover / scheduledDateTimeAtTransfer
RU_i ──Departure/ArrivalInterchangeReport──▶ RU_{i+1}
```

What the graph *does* know: every party by company code, every message, every border point, the train number, the vehicles, the sections of line and their GSM-R network. What it *does not* know: the LRU as a role, the path as an object, the C-OSS as an authority, any FRMCS attribute, and any public operator.

## 3. Where the public operator enters — three relations, three answers

**Relation A — path booking: the MNO is not a party.** No message in TD100 has a telecom operator as sender or receiver; TD100 §2.4's transport assumption is "any TCP/IP network" between RU/IM/LRU systems. The bearer is below the telematics plane by design (E-2026-09-19-24). *Interface:* none. *Ontology:* none needed.

**Relation B — the train's radio on the IM's network, today: the GSM-R roaming relation.** A cross-border path rides on `radioNetworkId` per section and on `gsmrNetworkCoverage` — the IM's own GSM-R network plus the networks its roaming agreement covers. This is an IM↔IM relation between *railway* networks (40 named in the scheme), registered by law in RINF. A public MNO appears here only as the network a driver's portable terminal or public phone may use to reach a fixed subscriber (Ril 481.0205 §9(5)); DB's own fallback rule for written orders (Ril 481.0101 §1(2)) is a person-to-person rule, not a network relation. *Interface:* GSM-R roaming (CS), registered in RINF. *Ontology:* `era:gsmrNetworkCoverage` + `GSMRNetworks` — exists, GSM-R-only. **Added 2026-09-20 (E-2026-09-20-01, UIC brochure Dec 2020, B-weight, 2026 state unverified — B19):** the relation is not purely bilateral. International GSM-R traffic, signalling included, passes through UIC-operated *interconnecting hubs* located in Germany with a back-up in Switzerland, linking 17 countries; UIC's 2020 plan says these hubs "must be upgraded to FRMCS". So a cross-border path has a third bearer party the register plane does not name — no `OrgRoles` term, no RINF parameter — and its FRMCS successor (interconnection between MC systems, TS 23.280/23.283; parity-suite MT-5 "which system arbitrates at the border") has no named owner in anything held.

**Relation C — FRMCS, where the IM may contract a public operator.** UIC V2 excludes MOCN and puts gateway redundancy out of scope (E-2026-09-19-19); DB's stated fallback direction is the public mobile network (E-2026-06-27-02); the 5G-RACOM hybrid is an operator direction (E-2026-07-01-02). In every such arrangement the IM becomes the *customer* of a public network for part of the bearer, and the register's R4 condition applies: the public path must re-provide the mission-critical functions (functional alias, group and emergency call, pre-emption) or it is not a safety-critical substitute. *Interface:* commercial (SLA) + technical (roaming / network sharing / the FRMCS gateway's multi-bearer policy, SRS §12.3) + management (TMF921 intent between the IM as AN consumer and the MNO's domain; TMF639 for the shared resources). *Ontology:* on the ERA side **nothing** — no MNO role, no FRMCS network term, no "covered by public network X" parameter, and the v4.0.0 RC closes the RINF shapes so none can be added informally (E-2026-09-19-44); on the TM Forum side the whole relation is expressible (SID `PartyRole` customer/provider, TIO intent with `sec:` expectations, TMF639 `externalIdentifier` to a RINF section IRI) but with no railway term of its own. **The link between the two sides does not exist; ADR-002 rule (xi)'s rail extension model is where it would be written.**

## 4. What this map says for the register

1. **The corridor declaration (ADR-001 5(c)) has a home for every party except the two it most needs:** the FRMCS network and the public operator. Everything else — IM per section, border point, RU, vehicles, train number, GSM-R network and roaming — is an IRI today.
2. **The path is not an object.** Messages have classes; the allocation they produce has none. An oversight layer asked "which paths ride on the failed core" cannot answer from the graph without an operator-side object joining `PathConfirmedMessage` to the sections of line — a rule-(x) extension, not ERA's.
3. **The Lead RU and the C-OSS are missing roles** in an `OrgRoles` scheme that has 92 others. For a multi-IM freight path these are the two coordinating authorities. Candidate for the same ERA channel as the FRMCS terms (A3′ lineage) — parked with it.
4. **Identity crosses the map as a phone number.** `operationalTrainNumber` is defined by its GSM-R use; `trainContactDetails` is a string. The FRMCS functional alias has no term on any plane — ADR-001 5(a)'s home-system anchoring has to be declared somewhere the path messages can reach.
5. **The public operator is modelled exactly where the law is not, and not modelled where the law is.** That is the whole finding of `pivot-notes-2026-09-19-ontology.md` §1 in one relation.
