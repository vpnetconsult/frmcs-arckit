## Resolution 1 — Q-7 is **REJECTED AS FRAMED**, because the test the board set for itself fails

**Q-7 proposed** that ADR-001 record its Option-C fallback against the RSEG rule that *"if cloud services for safety relevant or related systems are required, the supplier shall only use on-premises cloud services."* The entry directed the board to **test whether the rule actually bites before carrying it.** It was tested. It does not.

**1a — ⚠️ THE CLAUSE DOES NOT REACH OPTION C, AND THE ERROR IS THE CHAIR'S OWN.** RSEG §2.2.1.2 governs **cloud services**. ADR-001 Option C is **"FRMCS hosted on public MNO 5G (MOCN / hosted core / MVNO)"** — a bearer and network-hosting arrangement. **The word "cloud" appears in ADR-001 ZERO times.** **R3f asserted that the cloud rule "constrains ADR-001's Option-C fallback." That was an over-connection: two arrangements that share a *principle* — third-party infrastructure serving a safety-relevant purpose — were treated as though one clause governed both. It does not.**

**1b — AND EVEN THE SHARED PRINCIPLE IS ALREADY HANDLED.** ADR-001 item 5(b)(ii), amended 2026-09-04, already provides that **fallback carries catalogue classes D and E only by default**, and that **classes A–C move to a fallback bearer only on an explicit, evidenced decision that the bearer meets their requirement — never automatically because the primary failed.** **So the safety-relevant traffic the principle would protect is already withheld from the third-party path by a rule adopted before this question arose. Q-7 would have amended a decision that had already answered it.**

**1c — no amendment is made to ADR-001. The ADR is not edited by this minute.**

## Resolution 2 — what the board records instead, because something real remains

**2a — a narrow confirmation, recorded here and NOT written into ADR-001** (there is nothing to change): Option C's **"hosted core / MVNO"** shape means the FRMCS **core network** would run on infrastructure the operator does not own. **The class restriction at item 5 is what keeps that defensible — so that restriction is now doing load-bearing work it was not originally written to do, and should not be weakened without returning to this reasoning.**

**2b — where the cloud rule DOES bite, it is already carried.** A **cloud-hosted oversight layer** is a cloud service for a safety-related purpose. That is ADR-002/ADR-012 territory, and **ADR-012 item 2's gate already carries the on-premises-cloud rule from ARB-2026-09-05 R3b.** No further act is required.

## Resolution 3 — the process note, which is the session's real value

⚠️ **Q-7 existed for less than three hours and its outcome was to reject its own premise. The board records this as the queue working as intended, not as wasted effort:** R3f split the item out precisely *because* a ratified decision was being priced by an amendment to a different ADR, and the entry directed a test rather than assuming the answer. **Had the amendment been folded into ADR-012's text in the usual way, ADR-001 would now carry a constraint derived from a clause that does not govern it. The R1c rule adopted this morning caught its first error on its first use.**

**No requirement moved. ADR-001 stays `Accepted` and unedited. Register unchanged at 9 of 11 ratified.**
