## Resolution 1 — ⚠️ the board first splits the proposition, because it is two claims, not one

Q-8 as queued bundles a **structural** claim and an **empirical** one. They rest on completely different evidence and the board declines to rule on them together.

**(A) THE STRUCTURAL CLAIM — verifiable by reading the register, requiring no driver evidence at all:**
- **ADR-002's guardrail governs AUTHORITY** — *agents advise, never actuate*. It says nothing about the **FORM** the advice takes.
- **ADR-002 item 3 governs one acceptance failure mode — SURVEILLANCE.** It requires output addressed to a named human role rather than a management dashboard about that role, states the purpose as shared learning, and treats the layer as augmenting the *Fachreferent:in Betriebssicherheit* rather than substituting for it. **It does not address a second, distinct acceptance failure mode: INSTRUCTION — a system that tells a competent professional how to do their job rather than giving them what they need to do it themselves.**
- **Item 3 already carries the principle that makes the gap matter:** *acceptance turns on the form of the control*, and **a layer the safety culture rejects fails however technically sound it is.**

**(B) THE EMPIRICAL CLAIM — resting entirely on six interviews reported in a conference abstract:** that users *do* reject instruction-framed systems; that they estimate usefulness as low; that they experience such systems as *"patronizing"*.

**⚠️ (A) is true whether or not (B) is representative. The board rules on (A) and holds (B).**

## Resolution 3 — what is written, and what is not

**Carried into ADR-002 item 3:** a second acceptance failure mode named alongside surveillance — **INSTRUCTION**, the layer telling a competent professional how to do their job rather than giving them what they need to judge for themselves. **Recorded as an extension of item 3's existing principle (acceptance turns on form), not as an import of a new claim about users.**

⚠️ **NOT written, and expressly held:**
- **No claim that users reject instruction-framed systems.** The evidence is **six interviews in a conference abstract with no method detail and no data**, and it must never be cited as a finding about what train drivers think.
- **No re-examination of item 3's agent roster is directed.** The observation that the roster reads in the language of instruction — *"proposes"*, *"scores"*, against the Risk Sentinel's *"surfaces"* — is recorded in the evidence log and is **left as an observation, not converted into a work item on this evidence.**
- **The guardrail is untouched.**

**Follow-up, not an obligation: if a second, independent source on user acceptance of rail advisory systems arrives, the empirical claim (B) returns to the board — and only then does the roster question become live.**
