# Pivot notes — the ontology layer: FRMCS-side and TM Forum-side

**Date:** 2026-09-19 (late) · **Pivot state:** `current/` at 530 rows (`E-2026-09-19-50` last); baseline `2026-09-19_2321` is the day's frozen state. · **Siblings:** `pivot-notes-2026-09-15-knowledge-plane.md` (what the agents reason over), `13-legal-binding-chain.md` (what binds what, by index and version), `sdo-mapping-frmcs-gsmr-5gsa.md` (the SDO stack).
**Purpose:** one place that says, layer by layer, **which machine-readable vocabulary exists, who governs it, and which law it stands under** — for the rail/FRMCS side and for the TM Forum side — so the register stops treating "ontology" as one thing. This first section defines the layers **for ontology and law only**; the threads that use them come later.

**Outcome anchor:** *safe, continuous rail operations.* An ontology matters here for one reason: on 23 June the record of what failed, where, and under which rule could not be written in any form a machine — or a regulator's system — could check. The layers below say where such a form exists today, where it is coming, and where there is nothing.

**Guardrail, restated:** oversight, not control. A vocabulary grounds advice and records; it never grounds an actuation the human did not command (ADR-002).

**Method note:** "ontology" is used strictly — an RDF/OWL/SKOS/SHACL artefact with IRIs, published and governed. Information models that are not RDF (3GPP NRM, ASN.1 message sets, XML schemas) are named as such and not promoted.

## 1. The high-level layers — ontology and law only

Two columns are deliberately separate. **Law** is what an authority can hold someone to. **Ontology** is what a machine can validate a record against. The register's finding across nineteen days of reading is that the two columns meet at exactly one layer (L7, and only for registers, authorisation and — in draft — safety reporting), and that the FRMCS specifications sit in a layer with law above them and no ontology of their own.

### What the layering says, in four lines

1. **Law and ontology coincide at L7 only — for registers (RINF/ERATV/EVR/ERADIS), for vehicle authorisation (va-m2m, in prototype) and, in draft, for safety reporting (ISS).** Nowhere else does a legally handled vocabulary exist. The ERA Ontology is the only vocabulary on the whole stack that an authority has published as a technical document with a legal base.
2. **FRMCS has law above it (Table A2 binds the documents) and no ontology of its own below it.** The specification set is bound by index and version, not by term. Every FRMCS-named IRI that exists today is ERA's, and there are three of them (one TSI section, two ISS on-board codes). The RINF radio terms, the RCC checks and the ISS trackside codes are GSM-R-shaped by construction.
3. **The TM Forum side has ontology without law.** The TIO is the richest governed RDF vocabulary on the stack and is open by design to extension models from any SDO or operator — which is what rule (xi) of ADR-002 uses — but nothing in it binds anyone, and it knows no railway. Its rail extension would have to be proposed *into ERA's* framework (an extension branch) to acquire a legal handle, because ERA's is the only vocabulary governance with one.
4. **3GPP is machine-readable but not ontological at either layer it occupies.** NRM DNs and intent JSON are the objects the oversight layer will actually touch; they reach an ontology only through TM Forum's mapping (IG1253 §21.1) and through the operator's own extension under ERA's principles (ADR-002 rule (x)).

### The gap the threads will work from

Between L7 and L2′ there is a **vocabulary void three layers deep (L5, L4, L3)** exactly where FRMCS is specified. The register's positions that need an FRMCS term — the corridor declaration (ADR-001 5(c)), the on-board authorisation file (ADR-013 item 3), the post-incident record (ADR-012 item 5), the parity suite (`15-mcx-parity-suite.md`) — all carry FRMCS in prose today. Whether the term should come from above (ERA, by extension or core change) or from beside (a TIO extension model) is the question the threads take up.
