The one-gate principle remains right as GOVERNANCE — one decision point, one change record — but the two limbs feeding it produce incommensurable numbers, and ADR-011's change classification must not pretend otherwise.** **(b) THE HONEST FRAMING OF WHAT RISK ANALYSIS IS FOR — WORTH ADOPTING VERBATIM AS A PRINCIPLE.** Paraphrased: **there is no stand-alone "fire-and-forget" product that ensures protection against cyber-attacks, and no one-time integrable solution**; the asset must be **regularly** re-analysed against a **constantly changing** threat situation. **100% protection cannot exist**, because the threat picture changes and countermeasures must be drawn from **limited financial AND HUMAN resources**. **Therefore the task of risk analysis is to determine which risks to MITIGATE and which to ACCEPT**, yielding a **prioritised list of risk-reduction measures** — and **existing countermeasures must be mapped in, or the assessment is not valid.** **This is the same conclusion Katzenbeisser reached at the DZSF conference (E-2026-08-19-03) — cybersecurity cannot be demonstrated once at design and construction for all time — and the same resource constraint that conference named as binding. ADR-012 should carry the accept-vs-mitigate framing explicitly: its action items currently read as a list of things to DO, with no provision for recording what is knowingly accepted.** **(c) THE METHOD, AND WHY IT EXISTS.** Attack graphs derive from **attack trees** — the information-security technique for analysing threats and representing the conditions that must hold for a threat to become a successful attack — with **two deliberate departures from tree properties**: **(1) nodes need not all be connected, so INDEPENDENT attack paths onto one asset can be modelled**; **(2) dropping the "exactly one path between two nodes" rule allows attack sub-steps AND THEIR ASSESSMENTS TO BE REUSED**, where different chainings may lead to different consequences. Assessment is captured in an **attribute vector** which the tool **aggregates into an overall risk, taking account of already-established countermeasures and their risk-reducing effect**. Stated purposes: reduce **effort and error-proneness**, increase **traceability and expressiveness**. **(d) THE TOOLING RATIONALE IS ITSELF EVIDENCE ABOUT THE STATE OF THE FIELD.** The authors surveyed existing software — **both IT-security research tools and commercial or freely available products — and found NONE that fully covers the attack-graph requirements**, explicitly because **previous tools cannot adequately depict the complexity of the railway sector**. They therefore built one and released it open source (`incyde-gmbh/drawio-plugin-attackgraphs`, `INCYDE-GmbH/attackgraphs`). **For ADR-007 item 7 that means the security test surface does not need bespoke tooling procured from a vendor — and for R8/PR13 it is a small but real data point that the rail-specific security tooling market had a gap the public research programme filled openly.** **(e) SECTIONS NOT FULLY MINED IN THIS PASS, RECORDED HONESTLY:** §3 *Threat catalogues and threat mining*, §4 *Risk assessment* (the attribute-vector mechanics), §5 *Countermeasures* and §6 *Conclusion* were read only in outline. **If ADR-007 item 7 is built on this method, §3 and §4 must be read in full first — the threat-catalogue source and the attribute definitions are where the method's assumptions live.** **MOVES: `revise`.** **ADR-007 item 7** — the method reference now points at the primary method paper rather than at a secondary mention. **ADR-011 gains** the incommensurable-risk-calculus caveat on its change classification. **ADR-012 gains** the explicit mitigate-vs-accept framing. **NO STATUS FLIPS:** R14 **Open** · ADR-007/-010/-011/-012 **Proposed**.
