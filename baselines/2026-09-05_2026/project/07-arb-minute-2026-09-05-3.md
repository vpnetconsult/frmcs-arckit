## Resolution 1 — ⚠️ the chair's stated reason for NOT convening was wrong, and is withdrawn

The chair twice declined to convene on Q-6, on the ground that *"its revised proposal depends on item-1 accuracy evidence, which requires an estate that does not exist."*

**That conflated two different things.** Q-6's revised proposal is that the dissent metric **be declared uninterpretable alone and always reported alongside accuracy and calibration.** That is a **rule about how a metric is reported** — a decision about eval design, which is precisely what ADR-010 is for. **It does not require the data to exist. One can decide today that a number must never be published alone, with no instance of that number yet in hand.**

**The register already holds the distinction the chair failed to apply**: ADR-009's ratification records that *"the external dependencies gate the OUTCOME, not the DECISION."* **Q-6 was decidable from the day it was raised, and the delay was the chair's error, not a property of the item.** Recorded because the same error — treating an unmeasurable thing as an undecidable one — would silently park any number of eval decisions behind an estate that may never exist.

## Resolution 2 — the reporting rule. **Carried.**

**Dissent rate may never be reported alone.** Every report of item 2(a) carries, in the same view: **item 1's accuracy evidence** for the agent concerned, and **item 2(d)'s calibration cell**. **The reasoning is that dissent is a behavioural fact with at least three incompatible explanations — the human is rubber-stamping; the human is exercising judgement; or the advice is not worth following — and dissent alone cannot distinguish them. Calibration is what separates "followed because the advice was good" from "followed because following is easier."**

**Item 2(a)'s band reading stands but is subordinated:** near-zero and near-total remain signals, **but a signal to investigate, never a verdict on its own.**

## Resolution 3 — the promotion rule. **Carried.**

**A compliance or following rate may not be used as evidence of oversight quality at item 4's promotion gate.** ⚠️ **A high following rate is evidence about the human's behaviour, not about the system's merit, and treating it as quality evidence inverts the measure: it would reward exactly the rubber-stamping item 2 exists to detect.** Where an operational case is made for higher compliance — as FAS-D and FASaN both make — that case is a **business** argument and is recorded as such, never as an oversight-effectiveness argument.

## Resolution 4 — ⚠️ the third possibility, which the board takes as the session's substantive finding

FAS-D and FASaN both treat low compliance as a **risk to be engineered away**. ADR-010 item 2 treats low dissent as a **safety warning**. **Both presuppose the advice is worth following.** Six train drivers interviewed by DLR estimated rail ADAS's **overall usefulness as LOW** and preferred a system giving them information to adapt their own driving over one that — their word — **"patronizes"** them.

**The board records the failure mode this exposes, which no metric in ADR-010 currently detects:**

> **A useless advisory system produces excellent-looking oversight numbers.** If the advice adds little, competent humans correctly ignore it; dissent is high, calibration may even look sound, and every item-2 measure reads as healthy oversight — while the layer delivers nothing. **The eval framework can confirm that humans are exercising judgement without ever asking whether the system deserved their attention.**

**Consequence adopted: ADR-010 gains a standing question, not a metric — "is the advice worth following?" — answerable only by item 1's accuracy work against the item 5(d) reference system, i.e. against quantified human performance on the same task. No promotion may be granted while that question is unanswered, irrespective of how good the oversight measures look.**

⚠️ **Bound, binding on any citation:** the driver evidence is **six interviews reported in a conference abstract**. It is a signal that the question is open — **it is not a finding about what train drivers think, and must never be cited as one.**

## Resolution 5 — the information / instruction distinction is **routed, not decided**

The same source distinguishes **information the driver can act on** from **instruction they are expected to follow**, and rejects the second. **ADR-002's guardrail — "agents advise, never actuate" — protects against actuation and says nothing about whether the advisory layer patronises the people it advises.** This is currently recorded only in the evidence log (E-2026-09-04-24) and is **not** in ADR-002.

**ADR-002 is `Accepted`. Under ARB-2026-09-05 R1c the queue is the only write path, so this is OPENED AS Q-8 and is not written into ADR-002 by this minute.**
