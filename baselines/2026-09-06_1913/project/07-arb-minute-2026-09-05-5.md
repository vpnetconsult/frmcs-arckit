## Resolution 1 — the two readings of item 2(c)

| | Option | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Do nothing** — keep the automation-bias reading as the sole one; a safety register may legitimately prefer the conservative interpretation | **Rejected.** A conservative reading is defensible **only where the two cannot be told apart.** They can — see R2 — so preferring the pessimistic one would be choosing not to look |
| **1** | State both readings and name the discriminator | **Carried in part** — necessary but insufficient without R2's precondition |
| **2** | Demote 2(c) from "signature" to "trigger for investigation" | **Carried in part** — the demotion is right; the word "signature" asserts a conclusion the data does not support |
| **3** | **(1) + (2) + require the readings be distinguished before any promotion** | **✅ CARRIED** |

**Carried (Option 3).** Item 2(c) ceases to describe falling latency with rising compliance as *the automation-bias signature*. **It becomes a TRIGGER carrying two candidate readings — automation bias, or familiarisation — with the discriminators named and a promotion gate attached.** The field evidence is directly on point: the same correlation was observed across 1,049 advised rides and read by the researchers as drivers *"familiariz[ing] with the system's functionalities better, and thus … improv[ing] their timing."*

**The two discriminators, both already in this ADR:**
- **Calibration (item 2(d))** — a **familiarised** human should get *better* at rejecting bad advice, because they have learned the system's failure patterns; an **automation-biased** human gets *worse*. Direction of travel in the bad-advice cell separates them.
- **Skill retention (item 2(e))** — a familiarised human retains unaided capability; an automation-biased one loses it.

## Resolution 2 — ⚠️ the precondition the queue entry did not anticipate, and it is the session's substantive finding

**Calibration discriminates only if there is bad advice in the stream to reject.**

**If the agent's advice is uniformly good, a familiarised human accepts all of it, the bad-advice cell is empty, and calibration cannot distinguish competence from capitulation. The better the system performs, the less able this framework is to tell whether its humans are still thinking.** That is a perverse property and it was not visible until the two readings were forced apart.

| | Option | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Do nothing** — accept that calibration may be unmeasurable in good conditions | **Rejected.** It would leave the discriminator nominally in place while silently inoperative exactly when the system looks healthiest |
| **1** | Rely on naturally occurring agent errors to populate the cell | **Rejected** — their arrival is not controllable, and a period without them is indistinguishable from a period without oversight |
| **2** | **Require KNOWN-BAD advice to be present in the evaluated stream — seeded, and drawn from ADR-007's surfaces** | **✅ CARRIED** |

**Carried (Option 2).** **Calibration measurement requires a known-bad-advice probe: instances whose incorrectness is established in advance, present in the stream the human is evaluating, at a rate and unpredictability sufficient that their absence cannot be assumed.** ⚠️ **The board records the obvious hazard and does not resolve it here: deliberately feeding a human bad advice to measure whether they catch it is an intervention with its own ethics and its own safety consequences, and it may not be permissible on class 3–4 decisions at all. Design that constraint into ADR-007's surfaces, where the probe would live — not into live operations by default.**

## Resolution 3 — the intention–behaviour gap. **Carried.**

The study found perceived usefulness correlated with compliance (**r = .64**) while **behavioural intention did not**, attributing the gap to social-desirability bias and the intention–behaviour gap literature.

| | Option | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Do nothing** — acceptance surveys are standard practice | **Rejected.** The register adopted a survey-based acceptance method into ADR-002 item 3 **yesterday**; leaving it unqualified would carry an instrument this evidence says does not predict behaviour |
| **1** | **Every stated-preference acceptance instrument carries a behavioural measure beside it, and neither is reported alone** | **✅ CARRIED** |

**Carried (Option 1).** **What people say they will do does not predict what they do.** Applies to ADR-010's oversight measures and to **ADR-002 item 3's FASaN-derived survey method**, adopted 2026-09-04. ⚠️ **ADR-002 is `Accepted` and this touches it — so the ADR-002 half is QUEUED as Q-10 and is not written by this minute.** The ADR-010 half is written here.

## Resolution 4 — ⚠️ the Q-8 trigger: the new evidence does not support the held claim, and points the other way

ARB-2026-09-05/4 R3 held Q-8's empirical claim — *that users reject instruction-framed systems* — pending **"a second, independent source."**

**The board rules that the trigger is NOT met, on TWO grounds, the second of which matters more than the first:**

1. **Not independent.** Same author, same institution. A stronger method from the same source is not corroboration.
2. ⚠️ **And the evidence does not support the claim in any case — it points against it.** Perceived usefulness was **moderately positive (M = 5.01 before, 5.33 after) and did not change with first-hand experience**; ease of use was rated **6.19**. **Drivers did not reject the system. What they said was subtler and is corroborated: they judged their own driving already efficient and thought the system useful for their COLLEAGUES rather than themselves.**

**Q-8's empirical claim therefore stays held — but the reason changes from "insufficient evidence" to "the better evidence points the other way." The board records the difference, because a claim held for want of evidence invites someone to go looking for support, while a claim contradicted by the best available evidence should not be revived without explaining this study away.**

**Nothing in ADR-002 changes: the structural half carried at /4 R3 stands untouched, and it never depended on the empirical claim.**

## Resolution 5 — the pattern, and a rule earned from two instances

**Twice now a behavioural signal has entered an ADR carrying only its pessimistic interpretation** — dissent rate (Q-6) and latency/compliance (Q-9). Both were written by the chair; both were corrected only when outside evidence arrived.

**Rule adopted: where a BEHAVIOURAL signal is written into an evaluation ADR, the benign reading is stated alongside the adverse one, and the discriminator between them is named. Where no discriminator can be named, the signal is recorded as a trigger for investigation and never as a finding.** ⚠️ **The failure mode this addresses is specific: a safety register drifts toward pessimistic readings because they feel responsible, and a pessimistic reading asserted without its alternative is still an unevidenced assertion — the precise defect this register was built to catch, wearing safety-conscious clothing.**
