# Pivot notes — the ontology bridge between FRMCS and the TM Forum domain

**Date:** 2026-09-30 · **Pivot state:** `current/` at 609 rows (`E-2026-09-30-16` last); last frozen baseline `2026-09-27_2035` — today's rows are not yet frozen. · **Siblings:** `pivot-notes-2026-09-19-ontology.md` (the layers: which vocabulary exists under which law), `pivot-notes-2026-09-20-path-booking-ontology.md` (the parties of a cross-border path), `pivot-notes-2026-09-20-ibn-core-rail-e2e.md` (the intent stack; act A14), `19-sid-associations.md` (the SID on record), `17-open-autonomy-framework.md` §4 and §7 (the knowledge plane).
**Purpose:** say, object by object, **where an FRMCS thing lands in the TM Forum model, through which key the two are joined, and whether the landing place is finished** — so that "bridge" stops meaning a diagram and starts meaning a list of joins that can be written down and validated.

**Outcome anchor:** *safe, continuous rail operations.* The bridge matters for one reason: the oversight layer reads the bearer through an operations model (inventory, alarm, performance, intent) and must answer in railway terms (which section of line, which infrastructure manager, which train function). On 23 June those two descriptions of the same failure could not be joined by anything but people.

**Guardrail, restated:** oversight, not control. A bridge lets a record say *this alarm concerns that section of line*; it never lets a management-plane object command a railway one (ADR-002).

**Method note.** Three source rules are in force and are not re-argued here: ERA terms are extended and never redefined (ADR-002 rule ix); below register level the vocabulary is the operator's to build (rule x); the rail vocabulary on the intent side is a TIO extension model (rule xi). SID entities and attributes are cited from the GB922 Excel **v26.0**, SID associations from the browsable UML **R20.0** (CLAUDE.md, lead's rule of 2026-09-30). "Ontology" is used strictly, as in the 19 September note: the SID is a UML model, not RDF, and is named as such.

## 1. What the earlier notes left open

The 19 September note found a **vocabulary void three layers deep** (UIC FRMCS, ETSI TC RT, 3GPP MCX) with law above it and the TM Forum model beside it. The 20 September notes then worked the **intent side** of the bridge — the rail extension model of act A14 — and the **parties** of a path. What none of them had was the **inventory and assurance side**: which SID class an FRMCS object becomes, what that class is obliged to carry, and which TM Forum component would hold it. Today's reading supplies that (E-2026-09-30-01 … -09): the SID class relationships, the v26.0 data model, and the ODA component inventory with its statuses.

One result frames everything below. **The TM Forum model knows no railway and no FRMCS:** the v26.0 workbook has no entity for a mission-critical service, a functional alias, a train or a line, nor for a network slice, a SIM or a subscriber (E-2026-09-30-09). It offers generic hooks — resource, resource function, service, place, party role, digital identity — and the bridge is the act of saying which FRMCS object hangs on which hook.

**Corrected the same day (lead's pointer; E-2026-09-30-10).** The missing slice entity is not a gap. The mapping of 3GPP slicing onto the SID was worked in the ONAP modelling community with TM Forum's participation (2019): the slice *service* is a **customer-facing service**, managed at E2E service level and described with SID-based models; its realisation is resource management — RFS and resource function — and refers to 3GPP. The first draft of row 4 had the slice as a resource-facing service; that is ONAP's secondary example, not its main structure.

## 3. The five joins

The table reduces to five keys. Everything else is detail hung on them.

1. **Place ↔ RINF IRI** (row 3). A SID `Place` carrying an `era:SectionOfLine` or `era:OperationalPoint` IRI. Needs one operator-side statement per cell or site: *this resource covers that section*. The ibn-core note already named this term (an `NRCellDU` to the section it covers) and classed it an ERA extension-branch candidate, because it touches a register concept.
2. **PartyRole ↔ organisation role and company code** (row 1). Gives every resource's mandatory owner a railway identity.
3. **Resource ↔ 3GPP distinguished name** (row 2). Keeps the SID object and the 28.541 object the same thing under two descriptions.
4. **Alarm ↔ ISS occurrence** (row 8). The only place where a management-plane record could become a legally shaped safety record — once the draft act is adopted.
5. **Intent ↔ TS 28.312, with ERA IRIs as targets** (row 11). Act A14; not repeated here.

Joins 1–3 need no new vocabulary from anyone: they are identifiers carried across. Join 4 needs a mapping nobody has published. Join 5 needs the extension model.

## 4. Three limits on the TM Forum side, and one on the FRMCS side

1. **The relationships are old, and missing where they matter most.** Associations are read at R20.0 and carried six years forward by assumption. `Intent`, `ClosedLoop`, `Anomaly` and `AIModel` — rows 10 to 13, the oversight layer's own objects — have no relationship on record at all.
2. **The assurance components are the unfinished ones.** Inventory, catalog, location, identity, permissions and fault management are Specified; performance and anomaly are Prototype; intent, closed loop, root cause and trouble ticket are candidates without an identifier (E-2026-09-30-08).
3. **The SID is not an ontology.** It has no IRIs. A bridge written in RDF must either mint its own IRIs for SID classes — and say so — or stay inside the TIO, which covers intent and not inventory. Whether TM Forum publishes the SID in RDF is not on record here.
4. **The FRMCS side still has no terms of its own** at the three layers where it is specified. The bridge therefore runs from the TM Forum model to *ERA's* vocabulary and to *3GPP's* identifiers, and passes over FRMCS itself: functional alias, railway emergency call, the service classes and the multi-bearer policy cross only as untyped characteristics.

## 4a. A third party to the bridge: ONAP (added the same day; E-2026-09-30-10 … -12)

The lead's pointer: the mapping between 3GPP objects and the TM Forum model was worked in the ONAP community, not inside TM Forum alone. Three things follow from what was read there.

1. **ONAP is where the two models met in practice.** The slice-as-CFS reading (row 4) comes from its modelling sub-committee with TM Forum in the room. It is an orchestration platform's information model, not an ontology and not a standard; it sits on L2′ beside the SID.
2. **It has its own object names for rows 2 and 22** — virtual and physical network functions and their components — and its own path for rows 8 to 12: event stream → analytics → policy → controller (VNF/PNF Guidelines, Figure 1). No human role appears in that loop, and the one worked anomaly case on record uses it to remove subscribers on a model's output (E-2026-09-30-11). The bridge can take ONAP's observability requirements without taking its actuation path; ADR-002 requires exactly that split.
3. **The alarm of row 8 has a transport on record.** ONAP's event stream carries 3GPP fault-supervision, heartbeat, performance and provisioning notifications natively, by reference to 3GPP's own schemas (E-2026-09-30-13, R-408814). So the path 3GPP notification → collector → (SID `ResourceAlarm` on the inventory side) is implementable with published pieces; the step from there to an ISS occurrence is still the unowned one.
4. **What is read is old or partial.** The slicing proposal is 2019 and its diagrams are unread; the guidelines are substantively 2019 and the requirements 2021; the released ONAP model is not on record. Treat ONAP as a pointer to where the mapping lives, not yet as a source for any cell above except row 4.

## 5. What this says for the register

1. **The inventory half of the bridge can be written now, and the assurance half cannot be cited yet.** Rows 1–4, 8 and 15 rest on Specified components and recorded associations. Rows 10–13 rest on attributes alone.
2. **The mandatory owner is the most useful thing the SID brings.** ADR-011 asks that every loop be a configuration item with an owner; the SID makes exactly one owning party role mandatory on every resource, and a deployed model is a resource. The register's rule has a model behind it on the inventory side — and, for the loop itself, a class (`ClosedLoopPartyRole`) without a relationship.
3. **The alarm-to-occurrence mapping is the piece with a safety use.** It is where "what failed" in the operator's terms becomes "what happened" in the regulator's. It is also unowned: not TM Forum's, not ERA's, not 3GPP's.
4. **ODA conformance is a partial procurement lever.** It can be asked for where components are Specified and not elsewhere (R8; recorded in E-2026-09-30-08, no ADR asks for it today).
5. **Opinion, marked as such:** the smallest useful artefact is one alignment file stating joins 1–4 — about twenty SID classes given local IRIs, each tied to its ERA term or 3GPP identifier, with the mandatory associations as shapes — validated against one worked case, the 23-June occurrence. It would sit beside A14's extension model, not inside it. It is proposed as act A20; whether to build it is the lead's call.
